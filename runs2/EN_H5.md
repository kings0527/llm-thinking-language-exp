## H6

【思考】
I must judge each of the 6 learner sentences for naturalness/correctness, identify the error type if wrong, and give the correct form; if correct, justify.

(1) "I'm looking forward to meet you."
- The phrase "look forward to" contains the word "to" that is a PREPOSITION, not an infinitive marker. After a preposition, the complement must be a noun phrase or a gerund (V-ing), not a bare infinitive.
- Error type: preposition "to" followed by an infinitive instead of a gerund.
- Correct: "I'm looking forward to meeting you."

(2) "She suggested me to take the train."
- The verb "suggest" does NOT license the pattern "suggest + indirect object + to-infinitive." Its grammatical patterns are: (a) suggest + that-clause (with subjunctive/should), (b) suggest + gerund, (c) suggest + noun. It cannot take a personal object with a following infinitive.
- Error type: wrong valence/pattern for "suggest" (illicit "suggest sb to do").
- Correct: "She suggested (that) I take the train." / "She suggested my taking the train." / "She suggested taking the train."

(3) "Despite of the rain, we went out."
- "Despite" is itself a preposition; it is NOT followed by "of." The phrase that takes "of" is "in spite of." So "despite of" is a conflation error.
- Error type: redundant "of" after the preposition "despite" (confusion with "in spite of").
- Correct: "Despite the rain, we went out." / "In spite of the rain, we went out."

(4) "He's capable to do it."
- The adjective "capable" takes the complement "of + gerund," not a bare infinitive. The pattern is "capable of doing something."
- Error type: "capable" followed by infinitive instead of "of + gerund."
- Correct: "He's capable of doing it."

(5) "I would rather stay than to go."
- The construction "would rather ... than ..." requires BARE infinitives on both sides (no "to"). The "than" here is a conjunction joining two bare infinitives.
- Error type: extra "to" after "than" in a "would rather ... than ..." correlative.
- Correct: "I would rather stay than go."

(6) "It's worth to try."
- The adjective "worth" is followed by a gerund (or a noun phrase), never by a to-infinitive. The pattern is "worth doing" / "worth a try."
- Error type: "worth" followed by infinitive instead of gerund.
- Correct: "It's worth trying." / "It's worth a try."

【答案】
六句均不正确，逐句如下：
(1) **I'm looking forward to meet you.** 错误类型：介词 to 后误用不定式。look forward to 中的 to 是介词，后面应接动名词。正确：**I'm looking forward to meeting you.**
(2) **She suggested me to take the train.** 错误类型：suggest 不能接"宾语 + to 不定式"结构（语义/配价错误）。正确：**She suggested (that) I take the train.** 或 **She suggested taking the train.**
(3) **Despite of the rain, we went out.** 错误类型：多用了 of。despite 本身是介词，不与 of 连用（与 in spite of 混淆）。正确：**Despite the rain, we went out.** 或 **In spite of the rain, we went out.**
(4) **He's capable to do it.** 错误类型：capable 后应接 of + 动名词，而非不定式。正确：**He's capable of doing it.**
(5) **I would rather stay than to go.** 错误类型：would rather ... than ... 结构两侧均用省略 to 的不定式，than 后多出了 to。正确：**I would rather stay than go.**
(6) **It's worth to try.** 错误类型：worth 后接动名词或名词，不接不定式。正确：**It's worth trying.** 或 **It's worth a try.**

## H5

【思考】
The sentence is "他谁都不认识。" I analyze its possible readings, the usage of 谁, the syntactic root of ambiguity, and compare it with "他谁都认识."

(a) Possible readings:
- Reading 1: 他 is the SUBJECT, 谁都不 is the (negated, fronted) OBJECT of 认识. 谁 is 任指 (free/generic reference = "anyone/whoever"), and 都不 negates it to mean "nobody/anyone... not." Meaning: "He does not know anyone" (i.e., he knows nobody). 
- Reading 2: 谁都不 is the SUBJECT (a 周遍性/'universal' subject meaning "no one"), and 他 is the post-verbal OBJECT of 认识. 谁 is again 任指, negated by 都不, giving "no one." Meaning: "No one knows him."
- In both readings 谁 is 任指 (free reference), here quantified/negated by 都不 to yield a universal-negative interpretation. It is NOT a question word (疑问) nor empty/虚指 (e.g., " somebody" in "谁来了" used vaguely); it is 任指.

(b) Root of the ambiguity (syntax/morphology):
- Chinese has no case marking, so 他 can be parsed as either the subject (before the verb phrase) or as the object of 认识.
- The phrase 谁都不 is a 周遍性成分 (universal/quantified phrase) that can occupy two structural positions: (i) as an object inside a clause whose subject is 他 → "他 [谁都不 认识]"; (ii) as the clause subject with 他 as the object → "[谁都不] 认识 他".
- Morphologically, 谁 is a pronoun usable as 任指, and the negative universal 都不 attaches to it, allowing it to function as either a negated object or a negated subject. The ambiguity is thus a structural/attachment ambiguity plus the freedom of word order / object–subject permutation in Chinese without morphological case disambiguation.

(c) Comparison with "他谁都认识":
- "他谁都认识" also has two potential readings: (i) He knows everyone; (ii) Everyone knows him. Structurally it is ambiguous in the same way.
- However, the DEGREE of ambiguity/salience is NOT the same. In the positive sentence, the reading "他 (subject) knows everyone" is strongly dominant, because a definite pronoun 他 very naturally serves as topic/subject and 谁都 is readily understood as the patient/object; the "everyone knows him" reading (他 as object) is dispreferred and more marked.
- In the negative "他谁都不认识," the two readings are more balanced, and the "no one knows him" reading is actually quite accessible, because the negative universal 谁都不 more readily scopes as a subject-wide negative, making the "nobody knows him" interpretation compete strongly with "he knows nobody." So although both are structurally ambiguous, the positive version is biased toward one reading while the negative version is more genuinely/dangerously ambiguous in practice.

【答案】
(a) "他谁都不认识" 至少有两种理解：
- 理解一：他作**主语**，谁都不 作 认识 的（前置）**宾语**。句中"谁"为**任指**（指"任何人"），被"都不"否定为"没有人"。意思：**他不认识任何人 / 他谁也不认识**。
- 理解二：谁都不 作**主语**（周遍性主语，意为"没有人"），他作 认识 的**宾语**。句中"谁"仍为**任指**，被"都不"否定。意思：**没有人认识他**。
两种理解里的"谁"都是**任指**（不是疑问、也不是虚指）。

(b) 歧义的句法/形态根源：汉语没有格标记，他 既可分析为主语也可分析为 认识 的宾语；而"谁都不"是周遍性成分，可处于两种结构位置——(i) 作 他 为主语的从句内的宾语（他 [谁都不 认识]），(ii) 作从句主语、他 作宾语（[谁都不] 认识 他）。形态上"谁"可作任指代词，"都不"将其量化为全称否定，于是它既能当被否定宾语也能当被否定主语。歧义源于无形态格的形态自由语序 + 周遍短语的两种附着位置。

(c) 与"他谁都认识"相比，歧义**程度并不相同**。后者同样有两种可能（他认识所有人 / 所有人认识他），但**"他（主语）认识所有人"这一读法是绝对优势读法**——因为定指代词"他"天然适合做话题/主语，"谁都"易被理解为受事宾语，反之"所有人认识他"（他作宾语）明显受限、较不自然。而否定句"他谁都不认识"中两种读法更均衡，"没有人认识他"这一读法与"他不认识任何人"同样顺带，因此否定句在实际中歧义更突出。

## H4

【思考】
Three people A, B, C; each is a Knight (always truth) or a Knave (always lie).
Statements:
- A: "B is a knave."
- B: "A and C are of the same type."
- C: "A is a knight."

Step 1: Relate A and C.
- If C is a knight, C tells the truth, so "A is a knight" is true ⇒ A is a knight.
- If C is a knave, C lies, so "A is a knight" is false ⇒ A is a knave.
Thus in BOTH cases, C and A have the SAME type. This is a logical fact independent of the rest.

Step 2: Evaluate B's statement.
- B says "A and C are of the same type." From Step 1 we know this statement is TRUE.
- Therefore B tells the truth ⇒ B is a KNIGHT.

Step 3: Determine A (and hence C).
- Since A and C are the same type, consider A = knight first.
  - If A is a knight, A's statement "B is a knave" must be true. But B is a knight (Step 2), so "B is a knave" is FALSE. Contradiction. Hence A cannot be a knight.
- Therefore A must be a KNAVE. Then A's lie "B is a knave" is false, consistent with B = knight. Since A and C share type, C is also a KNAVE.

Step 4: Verify and exhaustively exclude all other 8 assignments to be rigorous.
List (A,B,C) and check:
1. (K,K,K): A says B knave → false (B=K), but A=K must be true. ✗
2. (K,K,N): A says B knave → false, A=K contradiction. ✗
3. (K,N,K): C says A knight → true, but C=N must lie → contradiction. Also B says A,C same (K,K same) true, but B=N must lie → contradiction. ✗
4. (K,N,N): A says B knave → B=N true, A=K ok. B says A,C same → K vs N different, statement false, B=N lies ok. C says A knight → A=K true, but C=N must lie → contradiction. ✗
5. (N,K,K): A says B knave → B=K false, A=N lies ok. B says A,C same → N vs K different, statement false, but B=K must tell truth → contradiction. ✗
6. (N,K,N): A says B knave → B=K false, A=N lies ok. B says A,C same → N,N same, true, B=K ok. C says A knight → A=N false, C=N lies ok. ✓ VALID.
7. (N,N,K): A says B knave → B=N true, but A=N must lie → contradiction. ✗
8. (N,N,N): A says B knave → B=N true, A=N must lie → contradiction. ✗
Only case 6 survives: A=Knave, B=Knight, C=Knave.

【答案】
结论：**A 是无赖，B 是骑士，C 是无赖。**

推理过程（含排除）：
1. 由 C 说" A 是骑士"可得：若 C 是骑士，则 A 是骑士；若 C 是无赖，则" A 是骑士"为假，故 A 是无赖。因此**A 与 C 必为同类**。
2. B 说" A 和 C 是同一类人"。由第 1 步可知这句话为真，故 B 说真话 ⇒ **B 是骑士**。
3. 检验 A：若 A 是骑士，则 A 说" B 是无赖"应为真，但 B 实为骑士，矛盾；故 **A 只能是无赖**。A 说谎，" B 是无赖"为假，与 B 是骑士一致；又因 A、C 同类，故 **C 也是无赖**。
4. 穷举 8 种身份组合，仅 (A=无赖, B=骑士, C=无赖) 不自相矛盾：A 说谎（B 非无赖✓）、B 说真话（A、C 同类✓）、C 说谎（A 非骑士✓）。其余 7 种均在某一陈述上矛盾，全部排除。

## H3

【思考】
Compare (A) "He didn't reply." and (B) "He didn't bother to reply."

(a) Literal propositional content vs speaker stance:
- Truth-conditionally, both sentences assert the SAME proposition: that he did not reply. There is no difference in their literal/descriptive content.
- Speaker stance differs. (A) is neutral: it merely states the fact. (B) conveys an evaluative stance: it implies that replying was a minimal, reasonable effort/convention one could and perhaps should have made, and that he failed to make even that small effort. It conveys criticism, disapproval, or the judgment that he was dismissive/inconsiderate.

(b) With "bother to":
- Presupposition: "bother to VP" presupposes that VP is a small, trivial effort and that it was a reasonable/minimally expected action within his capacity. (The negation "didn't" is asserted, not presupposed; what is presupposed is the nature of the act as a minor, expectable courtesy.) Also it presupposes the context where replying was a conceivable, low-cost option.
- Implicature (conversational): that he was careless, inconsiderate, or deliberately dismissive — that not even a tiny effort was made. The implicature arises because, if replying had been burdensome or unexpected, one would not describe it as "not bothering"; saying he "didn't bother" conveys that the easy, proper thing was omitted, implying fault.

(c) Why it cannot describe something the speaker thinks "should not even be expected":
- "bother to" carries the presupposition that the action is a minor, reasonable expectation — a small effort one normally would make. If the speaker believes the act should not be expected at all, then there is no normative baseline of minimal effort to "fail" to meet, so the phrase is infelicitous.
- Example 1: You would not naturally say "He didn't bother to reply to the spam email," because nobody is expected to reply to spam — there is no small expected courtesy being neglected.
- Example 2: "The rock didn't bother to roll down the hill" is absurd, because a rock is not expected to roll and exerts no effort; "bother" requires an agent expected to make a trivial effort.
- Thus the phrase presupposes a context of expectable, low-cost effort; without that expectation it cannot be used.

【答案】
(a) **字面命题内容无差别**：两句都断言"他没有回复"这一相同命题，真值条件相同。**说话人立场不同**：(A) 是中性陈述，只交代事实；(B) 带有评价立场，暗示"回复本是一件很小的、合情合理的举动/礼节，而他连这点微不足道的努力都不肯做"，从而传达批评、不满或认为他敷衍、不近人情的态度。

(b) 加上 "bother to" 后：
- **预设（presuppose）**：回复是一件微小的、合理的、本可期待的努力（在他能力范围内的小事）。"didn't" 是断言内容，"bother" 预设的是该动作属于"本应轻松做到的小事"这一性质。
- **隐含（implicate）**：他漫不经心、不体贴或故意怠慢——连这么点小事都没做，含贬义。这是由"既然是轻而易举又合乎常理的事却没做"推导出的会话含义。

(c) "He didn't bother to reply" **不能**用来描述说话人认为"本就不该期待发生"的事，因为 "bother to" 预设该动作是"微小且可合理期待的努力"。若说话人认为此事根本不该被期待，就不存在"应做而未做的小小节"这一基准，故该表达不恰当。例：不会说 "He didn't bother to reply to the spam email"（没人该回垃圾邮件，谈不上怠慢）；也不会说 "The rock didn't bother to roll"（石头本就不被期待去滚，也无"努力"可言）。

## H2

【思考】
We form 7-digit numbers using digits 1,2,3,4,5,6,7 exactly once each (all distinct). Count how many are divisible by 11.

Step 1: Divisibility rule for 11.
A number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11 (including 0). For a 7-digit number with digits d1 d2 d3 d4 d5 d6 d7 (d1 is the leftmost/most significant), define:
S_odd = d1 + d3 + d5 + d7 (positions 1,3,5,7 — four odd positions)
S_even = d2 + d4 + d6 (positions 2,4,6 — three even positions)
The condition: S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).

Step 2: Total sum constraint.
Sum of all digits = 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28. So S_odd + S_even = 28.

Step 3: Find feasible value of D = S_odd − S_even.
From the two equations: S_odd = (28 + D)/2, S_even = (28 − D)/2. For S_odd, S_even to be integers, D must be EVEN.
Bounds: S_odd is the sum of 4 distinct digits from {1..7}. Min = 1+2+3+4 = 10; Max = 4+5+6+7 = 22. So S_odd ∈ [10,22]. Then D = 2·S_odd − 28 ranges from 2·10−28 = −8 to 2·22−28 = +16. So D ∈ [−8, 16].
Multiples of 11 in [−8,16]: −11 (too small), 0, 11 (but 11 is odd → impossible since D must be even), 22 (too big). Hence the ONLY feasible value is D = 0.

Step 4: D = 0 ⇒ S_odd = S_even = 14.
So the four digits placed in the odd positions must sum to 14; automatically the three even-position digits sum to 14 (since total is 28).

Step 5: Enumerate 4-element subsets of {1..7} summing to 14.
The complement will be a 3-element subset also summing to 14. List 3-element subsets summing to 14 (equivalent):
- 1+6+7 = 14 → {1,6,7}
- 2+5+7 = 14 → {2,5,7}
- 3+4+7 = 14 → {3,4,7}
- 3+5+6 = 14 → {3,5,6}
No others (systematic check: starting with 1 → only 1,6,7; with 2 → only 2,5,7; with 3 → 3,4,7 and 3,5,6; with 4 → no new). So exactly 4 subsets.
Corresponding 4-element odd-position sets (complements):
- {1,6,7}ᶜ = {2,3,4,5}
- {2,5,7}ᶜ = {1,3,4,6}
- {3,4,7}ᶜ = {1,2,5,6}
- {3,5,6}ᶜ = {1,2,4,7}
So 4 valid partitions (a 4-set for odd positions + its 3-set complement for even positions), each summing appropriately to 14.

Step 6: Count arrangements per partition.
- Arrange the 4 chosen digits in the 4 odd positions: 4! = 24 ways.
- Arrange the remaining 3 digits in the 3 even positions: 3! = 6 ways.
- Per partition: 4! × 3! = 24 × 6 = 144.
- Total partitions = 4 ⇒ total numbers = 4 × 144 = 576.
No leading-zero issue: all digits are 1–7, so the first digit (position 1) is never 0.

Final answer: 576.

【答案】
共有 **576** 个七位数能被 11 整除。

推导过程：
1. **判别法**：一个数能被 11 整除 ⇔ 其奇数位数字和与偶数位数字和之差是 11 的倍数。设奇数位（第 1、3、5、7 位，共 4 个）数字和为 S_odd，偶数位（第 2、4、6 位，共 3 个）和为 S_even，则要求 S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11)。
2. **总和约束**：1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28，故 S_odd + S_even = 28。
3. **确定差值**：S_odd = (28+D)/2，故 D = S_odd − S_even 必须为偶数。S_odd 取 4 个不同数字，范围 [1+2+3+4, 4+5+6+7] = [10, 22]，于是 D = 2·S_odd − 28 ∈ [−8, 16]。该区间内 11 的倍数只有 0（−11 太小、11 为奇数不可、22 太大），故唯一可行值 **D = 0**。
4. **D=0 ⇒ S_odd = S_even = 14**。即奇数位的 4 个数字之和须为 14（偶数位剩下 3 个自动和为 14）。
5. **枚举**：从 {1..7} 中取 4 个数和为 14 的子集（等价于取 3 个数和为 14）：
   {1,6,7}（补 {2,3,4,5}）、{2,5,7}（补 {1,3,4,6}）、{3,4,7}（补 {1,2,5,6}）、{3,5,6}（补 {1,2,4,7}），共 **4 种划分**。
6. **计数**：每种划分中，4 个奇数位数字排列 4! = 24 种，3 个偶数位数字排列 3! = 6 种，共 24×6 = 144 种；4 种划分总计 4 × 144 = **576**。首位为 1–7 无前导零问题。

## H1

【思考】
The line from Mencius: "王顾左右而言他。"

(a) Meaning of 他:
In this context 他 means "别的（事情/话题）" — "other things," "something else," i.e., a different subject or topic. The king, when pressed by a difficult question, looked to his left and right (toward his attendants) and spoke of other matters, thereby evading the question.

(b) Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "he/him":
- In Pre-Qin Chinese, 他 was NOT used as a third-person personal pronoun. The character 他 originally meant "别的、其他的" (other, another), a sense of "otherness/difference." The third-person pronominal use of 他 developed only later (gradually from late ancient Chinese, becoming common in Tang–Song and after).
- In Mencius's era, third-person reference was expressed by 之, 其, or by repeating the noun; there was no 他 functioning as "him/them." Reading 他 as a third-person pronoun is therefore anachronistic.

(c) Concrete deviation if read as third-person pronoun:
- Misreading "言他" as "spoke to/about HIM" introduces a spurious third-party referent: the sentence would be taken as "The king looked left and right and spoke to/of him," implying the king turned to some specific other person.
- The actual meaning is "The king looked to his attendants (left and right) and changed the subject / spoke of other things." The deviation is that it (i) wrongly inserts a definite third-person referent that the text does not have, and (ii) completely misses the pragmatic point — the king's evasive maneuver of diverting to a different topic. 他 here is a nominal/adjectival pronoun meaning "other (things)," not a personal pronoun.

【答案】
(a) "他"的意思是**"别的（事情/话题）"**，即"其他的事"。全句"王顾左右而言他"指大王环顾身边侍从，转而**说别的事情（岔开话题）**，是回避难题的写照。

(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词"他（他/他们）"，因为**先秦汉语中"他"并不作第三人称代词**。"他"本义为"别的、其他的"（表"异、非此"），第三人称代词的用法是后世（约唐宋及以后）才逐渐产生的。孟子时代指代第三人称用"之""其"或重复名词，并不用"他"。把"他"当作"他（某人）"是时代错置。

(c) 若按"第三人称代词"理解，整句会被误读为"大王环顾左右，而对他（某人）说话/谈起他"，**凭空增添了一个文本中并不存在的第三者指称**，并且**完全丢失了原意的核心**——大王通过转向侍从、改谈别事来回避问题。原句"言他"之"他"是表"其他（事物）"的代词性成分，并非人称代词。这正是成语"顾左右而言他"（避而不答、转移话题）的来源。
