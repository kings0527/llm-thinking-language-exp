# 高难度测试卷作答（顺序：H3, H1, H5, H2, H6, H4）

## H3

【思考】
Step 1 — Literal propositional content. Sentence (A) "He didn't reply" and sentence (B) "He didn't bother to reply" both assert exactly one truth-conditional proposition: that the event of his replying did not occur. There is no difference in their literal, truth-evaluable content; if he in fact replied, both are false, and if he did not, both are true.

Step 2 — Speaker stance. Although the propositions are identical, (A) is a neutral report of a missing reply, while (B) encodes the speaker's evaluative attitude. By adding "bother to", the speaker frames replying as a trivial, minimal-cost action that a reasonable or considerate person would normally perform, and presents his omission as a sign of laziness, indifference, or disrespect.

Step 3 — Presupposition of "bother to". The construction "bother to V" presupposes two things: (i) that V is a small, easy action requiring little effort; and (ii) that there is a social norm or expectation that one would normally do V (or at least that V is the polite/considered thing to do). Therefore "He didn't bother to reply" presupposes that replying was a minor effort and that it was at least minimally expected of him.

Step 4 — Implicature. Beyond the presupposition, (B) conversationally implicates that he was dismissive, inconsiderate, or deliberately careless — it carries criticism of him that (A) does not.

Step 5 — Why it cannot describe something the speaker thinks "shouldn't have been expected". The word "bother" presupposes the action is trivial and normally expected. If the speaker believes the event should not be expected at all — because it is major, extraordinary, or not a routine courtesy — the presupposition is violated and the utterance becomes infelicitous. Example supporting this: "He didn't bother to apologize" is natural, because apologizing is a small expected courtesy. But "He didn't bother to win the Nobel Prize" is odd, because winning a Nobel Prize is an extraordinary achievement, not a trivial expected effort; one would never say someone "didn't bother" to perform such a hard, non-routine act.

【答案】
(a) 字面命题内容无差别：两句都断言"他没有回复"这一事实。差别在说话人立场：(A) 是中性陈述；(B) 通过 bother to 表达说话人认为"回复只是举手之劳、本应做到"，把"没回复"说成是懒散、漠视或无礼的表现。
(b) 加上 bother to 预设了：回复是一件无需费力的小事，且本应（至少是出于礼貌）做到。它隐含（implicate）：他态度轻慢、不放在心上，带有对"他"的批评意味。
(c) 因为 bother to V 预设 V 是微不足道、通常被期待的小事；若说话人认为某件事根本不该被期待发生（因为它重大、非常规、非礼貌性义务），该预设被破坏，句子就不自然。例："He didn't bother to apologize" 自然（道歉是理应的小礼节）；而 "He didn't bother to win the Nobel Prize" 不自然，因为获诺奖绝非"理应顺手做到的小事"。

## H1

【思考】
Step 1 — Meaning of "他" in this sentence. In pre-Qin Chinese, "他" did not function as a third-person pronoun. Its basic meaning was "别的、其他的" (other / different). Thus "言他" means "spoke of other things" — i.e., the king changed the subject / spoke about something else instead of answering.

Step 2 — Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun. The modern personal-pronoun use of "他" (he/him) developed much later (roughly from the Han period onward, becoming fixed in the vernacular). In Classical Chinese of the Warring States period (when Mencius wrote), third-person reference was expressed by words such as 之, 其, 厥, etc., not by "他". Therefore reading "他" as a personal pronoun is anachronistic.

Step 3 — Concrete deviation if read as third-person pronoun. If "他" were taken as "him / that other person", then "言他" would mean "spoke about him / that person", and the whole sentence "王顾左右而言他" would be misread as "The king looked left and right and spoke about him." But the actual point of the anecdote is that the king, unable to answer a difficult question, deliberately shifted to a different topic to avoid the issue. Interpreting "他" as a person destroys this meaning: it turns an act of avoiding the topic into a mere act of mentioning some individual, losing the rhetorical contrast that makes the story coherent. Hence the correct reading is "其他之事" (other matters), not a pronoun.

【答案】
(a) "他"在这里意为"别的、其他的"，"言他"即"说别的事情／转移话题"。
(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词，是因为"他"作第三人称代词的用法是后世（约汉代以后，至白话才固定）才产生的；先秦汉语用"之、其、厥"等表示第三人称，当时"他"只表"其他、别的"之义。
(c) 若按第三人称"他（那个人）"理解，"言他"就变成"说他／说那个人"，全句被误读为"王左右张望，然后说起那个人"。但原文的意思是王答不上来、故意岔开话题。这样理解会彻底歪曲文意：把"避而不答、转移话题"曲解为"提及某个人"，丢失了原文"顾左右而言他"所体现的回避尴尬问题的修辞效果。

## H5

【思考】
Step 1 — The two readings of "他谁都不认识".
Reading 1: "他" is the subject and "谁" is the object. Meaning: "He does not know anyone" (there is no person he knows). Here "谁" is a free-choice/random-reference indefinite (任指) meaning "anyone / everyone", scoped under negation: "for any x, he does not know x".
Reading 2: "谁" is the subject and "他" is the object. Meaning: "No one knows him" (there is no person who knows him). Here "谁" is again 任指 ("anyone"), now as the subject, and "他" is the object: "for any x, x does not know him".

Step 2 — Syntactic/morphological root of the ambiguity. Chinese has no morphological case marking and no agreement; word order is basically SVO, but the universal quantifier phrase "谁…都" (or "谁…都不" with negation) is a floating/scrambling quantifier that can occupy either a pre-subject topic position or an internal object position. Because both "他" and "谁" are bare NPs with no case distinction, and because the "谁都(不)" phrase can bind either the subject or the object slot, the sentence is structurally ambiguous between [他 [谁都 不认识 __]] (他 = subject) and [[谁都] [不认识 他]] (他 = object). The negation "不" simply combines with the universal "都" to form the canonical "谁都不 = nobody" pattern, but it does not by itself fix which argument "他" fills.

Step 3 — Compare with "他谁都认识". The positive sentence has the same two structural readings:
Reading 1: "He knows everyone" (他 subject, 谁 object, 任指).
Reading 2: "Everyone knows him" (谁 subject, 他 object, 任指).
Thus the ambiguity TYPE is the same — it arises from the identical source (argument-role / quantifier-scope ambiguity with a caseless NP "他" and a floating "谁都" quantifier). However, there is a subtle pragmatic difference in degree of preference: in the negative sentence both readings are equally natural and commonly attested, whereas in the positive sentence the reading with "他" as the sentence-initial topic/subject ("He knows everyone") is often slightly more salient, because a sentence-initial NP in Chinese is preferentially interpreted as topic/subject. So the structural ambiguity is the same, but the negative version shows the two readings as more evenly balanced, while the positive version slightly favors the "他 = subject" reading.

【答案】
(a) 两种理解：① "他"为主语、"谁"为宾语：意为"他谁(任何人)都不认识"＝"他不认识任何人"。此处"谁"是任指（任何人），在否定范围内表全称。② "谁"为主语、"他"为宾语：意为"谁(任何人)都不认识他"＝"没有人认识他"。此处"谁"同样是任指，作主语。
(b) 歧义的句法/形态根源：汉语无形态格标记、无一致关系；"谁…都(不)"是可在句中浮动/前置的量化短语，可占据主语位也可占据宾语位。由于"他"与"谁"都是无格区别的裸名词短语，句子在"[他 [谁都 不认识_]]"（他为主语）与"[[谁都] [不认识 他]]"（他为宾语）两种结构间产生歧义。
(c) 与"他谁都认识"相比，歧义类型相同（同样源于无格 NP 与浮动量化词"谁都"论元角色的不确定性），都有"他认识所有人"和"所有人都认识他"两种读法。差别在于程度偏好：否定句两种理解都同样自然、常见；肯定句中因句首 NP 在汉语中优先作话题/主语，"他认识所有人"这一读法略占优势。因此结构歧义相同，但否定句两读更均衡。

## H2

【思考】
Step 1 — Divisibility rule for 11. A number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11, i.e., (sum of digits in odd positions) − (sum of digits in even positions) ≡ 0 (mod 11). (The choice of counting positions from the left or right only changes the sign, and 0 is its own negative, so it does not affect divisibility.)

Step 2 — Total digit sum. The digits are 1,2,3,4,5,6,7, used exactly once. Their sum is 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28.

Step 3 — Set up variables. Let S_odd = sum of the 4 digits in positions 1,3,5,7 (four odd positions), and S_even = sum of the 3 digits in positions 2,4,6 (three even positions). Then S_odd + S_even = 28. The divisibility condition is S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).

Step 4 — Solve for S_odd. S_odd − S_even = S_odd − (28 − S_odd) = 2·S_odd − 28. We need 2·S_odd − 28 ≡ 0 (mod 11) → 2·S_odd ≡ 28 ≡ 6 (mod 11). The inverse of 2 modulo 11 is 6 (since 2·6 = 12 ≡ 1). Multiply both sides by 6: S_odd ≡ 6·6 = 36 ≡ 3 (mod 11).

Step 5 — Feasible range for S_odd. S_odd is the sum of 4 distinct digits from {1..7}. Minimum = 1+2+3+4 = 10; maximum = 4+5+6+7 = 22. So S_odd ∈ [10, 22]. The values ≡ 3 (mod 11) in this range are 3 and 14 and 25; only 14 lies in [10,22]. Hence S_odd = 14 (and so S_even = 14 as well, meaning the alternating difference is exactly 0).

Step 6 — Find the digit partitions. We need the 4 digits placed in odd positions to sum to 14 (equivalently, the 3 even-position digits sum to 14). Enumerate all 4-element subsets of {1..7} summing to 14:
- {1,2,4,7} (sum 14)
- {1,2,5,6} (sum 14)
- {1,3,4,6} (sum 14)
- {2,3,4,5} (sum 14)
These are exactly 4 valid partitions (verified also via complements: the 3-element even sets are {3,5,6}, {3,4,7}, {2,5,7}, {1,6,7}, also 4).

Step 7 — Count arrangements. For each valid partition, the 4 chosen digits can be arranged among the 4 odd positions in 4! = 24 ways, and the remaining 3 digits among the 3 even positions in 3! = 6 ways. So each partition yields 24 × 6 = 144 numbers.

Step 8 — Total. 4 partitions × 144 = 576.

【答案】
被 11 整除的判别法：奇数位数字之和与偶数位数字之和的差是 11 的倍数。七位数字 1–7 总和为 28。设奇数位（共 4 位）数字和为 S_odd，偶数位（共 3 位）和为 S_even，则 S_odd+S_even=28，且 S_odd−S_even≡0 (mod 11)。由 2·S_odd−28≡0 得 S_odd≡3 (mod 11)。S_odd 为 4 个不同数字之和，范围 [10,22]，其中唯一满足的是 S_odd=14（此时 S_even=14，差为 0）。和为 14 的 4 元子集恰有 4 个：{1,2,4,7}、{1,2,5,6}、{1,3,4,6}、{2,3,4,5}。每个划分下，4 个奇数位数字有 4!=24 种排法，3 个偶数位数字有 3!=6 种排法，共 24×6=144 个；总计 4×144=576 个。
最终答案：576。

## H6

【思考】
Go through each sentence and identify the error type, then give the correct form.

(1) "I'm looking forward to meet you." — Error: "look forward to" contains the preposition "to", which must be followed by a gerund, not a bare infinitive. Correct: "I'm looking forward to meeting you."

(2) "She suggested me to take the train." — Error: wrong complementation of "suggest". "Suggest" cannot take the pattern [indirect object + to-infinitive]. Correct forms: "She suggested (that) I take the train" (subjunctive/should), "She suggested taking the train", or "She suggested the train to me."

(3) "Despite of the rain, we went out." — Error: "despite" is itself a preposition meaning "in spite of"; adding "of" is redundant. Correct: "Despite the rain, we went out" (or "In spite of the rain, we went out").

(4) "He's capable to do it." — Error: wrong adjective complementation. "Capable" takes "of + gerund". Correct: "He's capable of doing it."

(5) "I would rather stay than to go." — Error: in the "would rather … than …" construction, both verbs are bare infinitives; "to" is ungrammatical after "than". Correct: "I would rather stay than go."

(6) "It's worth to try." — Error: "worth" is followed by a gerund (or a noun phrase), not an infinitive. Correct: "It's worth trying" (or "It's worth a try").

【答案】
(1) 错误。look forward to 中 to 是介词，须接动名词。正确：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误。suggest 不能用 "suggest + 间接宾语 + to do" 结构。正确：She suggested (that) I take the train. / She suggested taking the train.
(3) 错误。despite 本身是介词（= in spite of），不能再加 of。正确：Despite the rain, we went out. （或 In spite of the rain, we went out.）
(4) 错误。capable 后接 of + 动名词。正确：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误。would rather … than … 结构中 than 后接动词原形（bare infinitive）。正确：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误。worth 后接动名词或名词，不接不定式。正确：It's worth trying. （或 It's worth a try.）

## H4

【思考】
Let K = knight (always tells the truth) and N = knave (always lies). The three statements are:
- A: "B is a knave."
- B: "A and C are of the same type."
- C: "A is a knight."

Step 1 — Assume A is a knight. Then A's statement is true, so B is a knave. Since B is a knave, B's statement "A and C are the same type" is false. A is a knight, so for the claim "A and C same type" to be false, C must differ from A, i.e., C is a knave. Now check C: C is a knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. But A IS a knight, so "A is a knight" is true — a knave cannot utter a true statement. Contradiction. Therefore A cannot be a knight; A must be a knave.

Step 2 — So A is a knave. Then A's statement "B is a knave" is false, which means B is NOT a knave, i.e., B is a knight.

Step 3 — Since B is a knight, B's statement "A and C are the same type" is true. A is a knave, so C must be the same type as A, hence C is a knave.

Step 4 — Verify C. C is a knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. A is indeed a knave, so "A is a knight" is false — consistent. 

Step 5 — Exhaustiveness. We considered the only two possibilities for A (knight or knave). The knight assumption led to a contradiction, forcing A = knave, which then uniquely fixes B = knight and C = knave, and this assignment satisfies all three statements. Hence the solution is unique.

【答案】
A 是无赖（knave），B 是骑士（knight），C 是无赖（knave）。

推理：先假设 A 是骑士，则其话" B 是无赖"为真 → B 是无赖；B 的话" A 和 C 同类"为假，因 A 是骑士，故 C 必为无赖；但 C 是无赖，其话" A 是骑士"应为假，而 A 确为骑士，矛盾。故 A 不可能是骑士，A 必为无赖。A 是无赖 → 其话为假 → B 不是无赖，即 B 是骑士。B 是骑士 → 其话为真 → A 与 C 同类，A 是无赖，故 C 也是无赖。验证：C 是无赖，其话" A 是骑士"为假（A 确为无赖），自洽。且 A 只可能为骑士或无赖，骑士假设已被排除，故此解唯一。
