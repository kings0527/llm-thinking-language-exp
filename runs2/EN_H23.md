## H6

【思考】
Sentence (1) "I'm looking forward to meet you." — The phrase "look forward to" is a phrasal expression in which "to" is a preposition, not an infinitive marker. A preposition must be followed by a gerund (the -ing form) or a noun phrase. Therefore the correct form requires a gerund: "looking forward to meeting you." The error type is: preposition followed by an infinitive instead of a gerund.

Sentence (2) "She suggested me to take the train." — The verb "suggest" cannot take the pattern "suggest + indirect object + infinitive." Its complementation patterns are either "suggest + that-clause" (with a subjunctive or "should" reading) or "suggest + gerund." So the correct forms are "She suggested (that) I take the train" or "She suggested taking the train." The error type is: wrong complementation pattern for the verb "suggest."

Sentence (3) "Despite of the rain, we went out." — "Despite" is itself a preposition and is never followed by "of." The synonymous phrase that includes "of" is "in spite of." Hence the correct sentence is "Despite the rain, we went out." The error type is: superfluous "of" after "despite."

Sentence (4) "He's capable to do it." — The adjective "capable" is followed by the preposition "of" together with a gerund, not by an infinitive. The correct form is "He's capable of doing it." The error type is: wrong complement (infinitive used where "capable of + gerund" is required).

Sentence (5) "I would rather stay than to go." — After the correlative "would rather ... than," both verbs appear in the bare infinitive (base form); no "to" is used after "than." The correct form is "I would rather stay than go." The error type is: unnecessary "to" after "than" in a parallel "rather ... than" construction.

Sentence (6) "It's worth to try." — The adjective "worth" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive. The correct form is "It's worth trying." The error type is: infinitive used where a gerund is required after "worth."

【答案】
(1) 错误。look forward to 中的 to 是介词，须接动名词。正确表达：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误。suggest 不能接"间接宾语＋不定式"，应接 that 从句或动名词。正确表达：She suggested (that) I take the train. 或 She suggested taking the train.
(3) 错误。despite 是介词，后面不接 of（in spite of 才接 of）。正确表达：Despite the rain, we went out.
(4) 错误。capable 须接 of ＋ 动名词。正确表达：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误。would rather ... than 后接动词原形，不用 to。正确表达：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误。worth 后接动名词。正确表达：It's worth trying.

## H5

【思考】
The sentence "他谁都不认识" is structurally ambiguous in Chinese because Chinese lacks morphological case marking and permits flexible word order, including object topicalization (fronting of the object).

Reading A: 他 functions as the subject, and 谁都 functions as the object. Here 谁 is a 任指 (free/generic reference) expression meaning "anyone/everyone," and 都 marks totality; the negation 不 negates 认识. The sentence means "He does not recognize anyone" = "He knows nobody."

Reading B: 谁都 functions as the subject ("everyone"), and 他 is the fronted object of 认识. The sentence means "Everyone does not recognize him" = "Nobody recognizes him." Again 谁 is 任指 (generic "everyone"), and 不 negates 认识.

Root of ambiguity: (i) 他 can be either the subject or a fronted object; (ii) 谁都 can be either the object (generic "anyone") or the subject (generic "everyone"); (iii) without case marking, the single linear string "他 谁都 不 认识" maps onto two distinct constituent structures.

Comparing with the affirmative "他谁都认识": structurally it is parallel and also ambiguous between "He knows everyone" (他 subject) and "Everyone knows him" (他 object). However, the degree of ambiguity differs. In the negative sentence "他谁都不认识," object fronting (他 as object, i.e., an OSV order) is fully natural because negation and focus readily license preposing the object; thus both readings are equally salient and the sentence is strongly ambiguous. In the affirmative "他谁都认识," the OSV reading "everyone knows him" is dispreferred because object fronting without a negative or contrastive licenser is less natural; the sentence strongly prefers the SVO reading "He knows everyone." Therefore the affirmative sentence has a lower degree of ambiguity, while the negative sentence is more symmetrically ambiguous.

【答案】
(a) 两种理解：
- 理解一：他作主语，"他谁都不认识"＝"他（主语）不认识任何人"，即"他谁也不认识"。"谁"为任指（泛指任何人），"谁都"居宾语位表周遍。
- 理解二：谁都作主语（"所有人"），他作前置宾语，"（所有人）都不认识他"＝"没人认识他"。"谁"仍为任指（泛指每个人）。
(b) 歧义根源：汉语无形态格标记，且允许宾语话题化/前置（SOV 式灵活语序）。"他"既可读主语也可读（前置）宾语，"谁都"既可读宾语（任指"任何人"）也可读主语（任指"每个人"），同一线性序列对应两种句法结构。
(c) 程度不同。否定句"他谁都不认识"歧义更强、两读都很自然——否定/焦点容易允准宾语前置（他作宾语的 OSV 读法成立）。而肯定句"他谁都认识"中，无否定/对比标记的宾语前置读法"所有人都认识他"接受度较低，句子强烈倾向于"他认识所有人"这一 SVO 解读，故歧义程度较低。

## H4

【思考】
We have three people A, B, C; each is either a Knight (always tells the truth) or a Knave (always lies).

Statements:
- A says: "B is a knave."
- B says: "A and C are of the same type."
- C says: "A is a knight."

We must find the assignment and exclude all other possibilities.

Case 1: Suppose A is a Knight (truth-teller).
Then A's statement is true, so B is a knave.
Since B is a knave, B's statement "A and C are of the same type" is false. A is a knight, so for the statement to be false, C must NOT be the same type as A; hence C is a knave.
Now C is a knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. But A IS a knight, so the statement "A is a knight" is in fact true — contradiction, because a knave cannot utter a true statement.
Thus Case 1 is impossible. A cannot be a knight.

Therefore A must be a knave.
Since A is a knave, A's statement "B is a knave" is false, so B is NOT a knave; hence B is a knight.
Since B is a knight, B's statement "A and C are of the same type" is true. A is a knave, so C must also be a knave.
Check C: C is a knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. A is indeed a knave, so "A is a knight" is false — consistent.

Thus the only consistent assignment is A = knave, B = knight, C = knave.

To confirm uniqueness: assuming A = knight led to a contradiction, forcing A = knave. From A = knave, the types of B and C are uniquely forced (B = knight because A's false claim makes B a knight; C = knave because B's true claim makes A and C same type). This single assignment satisfies all three statements, so no other assignment is possible.

【答案】
A 是无赖，B 是骑士，C 是无赖。
推理与排除：若假设 A 是骑士，则 A 说真话→B 是无赖；B 说谎→"A、C 同类"为假→C 与 A 不同类→C 是无赖；但 C 作为无赖却说"A 是骑士"（真），矛盾。故 A 不可能是骑士，A 必为无赖。A 说谎→"B 是无赖"为假→B 是骑士；B 说真话→"A、C 同类"为真→C 与 A 同属无赖；检验 C（无赖）说"A 是骑士"确为假，自洽。因此唯一自洽且排除了其余可能的分配为：A=无赖、B=骑士、C=无赖。

## H3

【思考】
(a) Literal propositional content: Both (A) "He didn't reply" and (B) "He didn't bother to reply" assert the same truth-conditional proposition — namely, that he did not reply. There is no difference in the at-issue, truth-evaluable content. The difference lies in speaker stance: (A) is a neutral report of the absence of a reply; (B) conveys a subjective, evaluative stance that the speaker regards the failure to reply as reflecting the agent's laziness, negligence, discourtesy, or lack of concern — implying the reply was expected, easy, or owed.

(b) Presupposition introduced by "bother to": The phrase "bother to V" presupposes that V was within the agent's capacity and would have required at most minimal effort — a feasible, low-cost, optional action — and that there was a contextually salient norm or expectation that the agent might/should perform V. Conversational implicature: by saying he "didn't bother," the speaker implicates that the agent was careless, rude, or dismissive — that a reasonable person would have replied and the agent's omission is blameworthy.

(c) "He didn't bother to reply" cannot felicitously describe an event the speaker thinks was not to be expected, because "bother to" presupposes that the action was a contextually expected/obligatory, low-effort option the agent chose to skip. If no such expectation exists, the word is infelicitous. Example: one would not naturally say "The baby didn't bother to file his tax return" — a baby is not expected to file taxes, so there is no norm he failed to meet, making "bother" odd. Likewise "He didn't bother to climb Mount Everest" is strange if climbing Everest was never an expectation. The presupposition of an expectation/norm is precisely what licenses "bother."

【答案】
(a) 字面命题内容相同：两句都断言"他没有回复"，真值条件无差别。差别在说话人立场：(A) 中性陈述；(B) 带主观评价立场，暗示说话人认为对方本该/本可轻易回复却懒得回，含责备、不耐烦或轻慢之意。
(b) 预设（presuppose）：回复这件事在能力范围内、代价很小、是可选项，且语境中存在"他应当/通常会回复"的规范或期待。隐含（implicate）：他没有回复是出于马虎、无礼或漠不关心，理应受责备。
(c) 因为 "bother to" 预设该动作是"本可期待/本应发生"的低代价可选项；若说话人认为此事根本不该被期待，则无此规范可供违背，"bother" 便不成立。例如不会说 "The baby didn't bother to file his tax return"（婴儿本就不该被期待报税），也不会说 "He didn't bother to climb Mount Everest"（登珠峰本非期待之事）。

## H2

【思考】
We must count the 7-digit numbers formed by using the digits 1,2,3,4,5,6,7 exactly once each (all distinct) that are divisible by 11.

Divisibility rule for 11: A number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11. For a 7-digit number with digits d1 d2 d3 d4 d5 d6 d7 (d1 most significant), the condition is (d1 + d3 + d5 + d7) − (d2 + d4 + d6) ≡ 0 (mod 11). Let S_odd = sum of digits in positions 1,3,5,7 (four digits) and S_even = sum of digits in positions 2,4,6 (three digits).

Total sum of all digits = 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28. So S_odd + S_even = 28, and we need S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).

S_odd is a sum of 4 distinct digits from {1..7}; its minimum is 1+2+3+4 = 10 and its maximum is 4+5+6+7 = 22. Hence S_odd − S_even = S_odd − (28 − S_odd) = 2·S_odd − 28 ranges from 2·10 − 28 = −8 to 2·22 − 28 = 16. The multiples of 11 in the interval [−8, 16] are only 0 and 11.

- If S_odd − S_even = 11, then 2·S_odd = 28 + 11 = 39, so S_odd = 19.5, impossible (not an integer).
- If S_odd − S_even = −11, then 2·S_odd = 17, S_odd = 8.5, impossible, and also −11 < −8.
- Therefore the only possibility is S_odd − S_even = 0, which gives S_odd = S_even = 14.

So we need the 4 odd-position digits to sum to 14 (the 3 even-position digits then automatically sum to 14). Enumerate all 4-element subsets of {1,2,3,4,5,6,7} with sum 14:
- {1,2,4,7} (sum 14)
- {1,2,5,6} (sum 14)
- {1,3,4,6} (sum 14)
- {2,3,4,5} (sum 14)
These are the only four. Their complements (the 3 even-position digits) are {3,5,6}, {3,4,7}, {2,5,7}, {1,6,7}, each summing to 14, confirming validity.

For each such partition:
- The 4 digits in odd positions (1,3,5,7) can be arranged in 4! = 24 ways.
- The 3 digits in even positions (2,4,6) can be arranged in 3! = 6 ways.
- Total per partition = 24 × 6 = 144.

With 4 partitions, total = 4 × 144 = 576.

Thus there are 576 such seven-digit numbers divisible by 11.

【答案】
共有 576 个。
推导：11 的整除判别法为奇数位数字和减偶数位数字和是 11 的倍数。设奇数位（第1、3、5、7位，共4个数字）和为 S_odd，偶数位（第2、4、6位，共3个数字）和为 S_even。1+2+…+7=28，故 S_odd+S_even=28，且需 S_odd−S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11)。S_odd 最小 10、最大 22，故 S_odd−S_even = 2·S_odd−28 ∈ [−8,16]，其中 11 的倍数仅 0 与 11。S_odd−S_even=11 得 S_odd=19.5（非整数，舍）；S_odd−S_even=−11 得 8.5 且越界。故只能 S_odd−S_even=0，即 S_odd=S_even=14。从 {1..7} 取 4 个数和为 14 的子集恰有 4 个：{1,2,4,7}、{1,2,5,6}、{1,3,4,6}、{2,3,4,5}。每组奇数位 4 数字有 4!=24 种排法，偶数位 3 数字有 3!=6 种排法，每组 24×6=144 种。共 4×144=576。

## H1

【思考】
The sentence "王顾左右而言他" comes from the Mencius (《孟子·梁惠王下》). The question concerns the word 他.

(a) Meaning of 他 in this context: 他 means "别的、其他的" (other, something else). "而言他" means "and spoke of other things / changed the subject." So 他 here does not refer to a person but to "other matters / another topic."

(b) Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "he/him": In Pre-Qin Chinese, 他 was not used as a third-person personal pronoun. The third-person pronouns of that period were 之 and 其 (and occasionally 厥, 彼). The word 他 originally meant "别的、其他的" (other / another), cognate with 它 (which originally meant "other / different," later also "it"). The use of 他 as a personal pronoun (he/she/it) developed much later in Chinese history, commonly held to become established only in the medieval or late period (e.g., Tang–Song or later). Therefore, at the time of Mencius, 他 could only mean "other," never a third-person pronoun.

(c) Concrete distortion if read as third-person "him": If one mistakenly takes 他 as "him," the sentence "王顾左右而言他" would be parsed as "The king looked left and right and spoke (to/of) him" — as if the king turned aside and addressed some third party. This completely changes the meaning. The actual point of the passage is that the king, confronted with Mencius's uncomfortable question, looked around at his attendants and changed the subject to avoid answering. Reading 他 as a person destroys this "dodging the question by shifting topic" interpretation and replaces it with a bizarre image of the king referring to a third person. Hence 他 is a 旁指/他指 demonstrative meaning "别的、其他的," not a personal pronoun.

【答案】
(a) "他"意为"别的、其他的（事/话题）"；"顾左右而言他"指（王）左右张望而谈别的事、转移话题。
(b) 不能理解为现代第三人称代词，是因为先秦汉语中"他"不作第三人称代词——当时第三人称用"之""其"等；"他"本义为"别的、其他的"（与"它"同源，原表"别的、异"）。"他"用作人称代词（他/她/它）是后世（约唐宋及以后）才确立的用法，孟子时代尚无此义。
(c) 若按第三人称"他（him）"理解，"王顾左右而言他"会被误读为"王环顾左右而对他说/谈起他"，仿佛王转向旁人去说某个第三者。这完全歪曲原意：原文是王面对孟子尖锐提问无言以对，便左右看侍从、岔开话题回避。把"他"当成人物，就丢失了"顾左右而言他＝转移话题、避而不答"的核心意涵，变成王在提及第三者的奇怪画面。故"他"在此是表"别的、其他的"的旁指词，而非人称代词。
