# 高难度测试卷作答（顺序 H6 → H1）

## H6

【思考】
I examine each of the six learner sentences one by one, identify the error type, and give the correct form.

(1) "I'm looking forward to meet you."
The fixed phrase "look forward to" is always followed by a noun phrase or a gerund, because the word "to" here is a preposition (meaning "toward"), not the infinitive marker. The learner used the bare infinitive "meet". Error type: preposition followed by infinitive instead of gerund. Correction: "I'm looking forward to meeting you."

(2) "She suggested me to take the train."
The verb "suggest" does not license the pattern "suggest + indirect object + to-infinitive". Its grammatical patterns are: (a) "suggest (that) someone (should) do something" (subjunctive or should-construction), (b) "suggest doing something", or (c) "suggest something to someone". Error type: wrong subcategorization frame of "suggest" (it cannot take object + infinitive). Correction: "She suggested (that) I take the train" or "She suggested taking the train."

(3) "Despite of the rain, we went out."
"Despite" is itself a preposition and is never combined with "of". The phrase that takes "of" is "in spite of". Error type: redundant "of" after "despite". Correction: "Despite the rain, we went out" or "In spite of the rain, we went out."

(4) "He's capable to do it."
The adjective "capable" is followed by "of + gerund", not by a to-infinitive. Error type: wrong complementation of "capable" (should be "capable of doing"). Correction: "He's capable of doing it."

(5) "I would rather stay than to go."
In the correlative construction "would rather ... than ...", both verbs are bare infinitives (no "to"). The "to" before "go" is ungrammatical. Error type: infinitive with "to" after "than" in "rather ... than". Correction: "I would rather stay than go."

(6) "It's worth to try."
The adjective "worth" is followed by a gerund (or by a noun phrase), never by a to-infinitive. Error type: infinitive instead of gerund after "worth". Correction: "It's worth trying" or "It's worth a try."

【答案】
(1) 错误：介词 to 后误用不定式。look forward to 中的 to 是介词，须接动名词。正确：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误：suggest 不能接“间接宾语 + to 不定式”结构。正确：She suggested (that) I take the train. 或 She suggested taking the train.
(3) 错误：despite 是介词，本身不能加 of（in spite of 才可加 of）。正确：Despite the rain, we went out. 或 In spite of the rain, we went out.
(4) 错误：capable 须接 of + 动名词。正确：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误：would rather ... than ... 后接动词原形（不带 to）。正确：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误：worth 须接动名词或名词，不接不定式。正确：It's worth trying. 或 It's worth a try.

## H5

【思考】
The sentence is "他谁都不认识。"

(a) There are two readings.
Reading 1: 他 is the subject and 谁 is the object. Meaning: "He doesn't know anyone / He knows nobody." Here 谁 is a free/generic indefinite pronoun (任指), meaning "anyone / whoever"; together with 都 and the negator 不 it forms the universally negative quantifier phrase 谁都不(认识) = "not ... anyone".
Reading 2: 谁 is the subject and 他 is the object, with 他 fronted to topic/object position. Meaning: "Nobody knows him." Here 谁 is again a free indefinite (任指) = "anyone / whoever", giving 谁都不认识 = "nobody knows", and 他 is the object of 认识.
In both readings 谁 is 任指 (free/generic reference), not a true interrogative (疑问) and not empty/vague reference (虚指).

(b) Root of the ambiguity: Chinese personal pronouns have no morphological case marking, so 他 is identical in nominative ("he") and accusative ("him"). Chinese also permits topic fronting and relatively free word order. The negator 不 scopes over the verb 认识, but subject vs. object grammatical relations are not morphologically distinguished. The universal phrase 谁都 can be analyzed either as the object of a clause whose subject is 他 (Reading 1), or as the subject of a clause whose object 他 has been fronted to topic position (Reading 2). With no case marking and no rigid configurational marking, both constituency analyses remain available.

(c) The affirmative counterpart "他谁都认识" is NOT equally ambiguous. Its dominant, essentially only natural reading is "He knows everyone" (他 subject, 谁都 object). The reversed reading "Everyone knows him" is much harder to access and remains marginal. Reason: in Chinese the sentence-initial position strongly biases toward a subject/topic reading, so 他 in first position defaults to subject. With the affirmative universal 谁都认识 ("everyone knows"), making 他 the object requires an object-topicalization that is pragmatically dispreferred, whereas in the negative sentence the universal negative 谁都不 ("nobody") is itself a natural sentential subject, so the "nobody knows him" reading is fully accessible. Hence the negated sentence is genuinely and robustly ambiguous, while the affirmative one is effectively unambiguous.

【答案】
(a) 两种理解：
① 他为主语、谁为宾语：意为“他谁也不认识 / 他一个人都不认识”。其中“谁”是任指代词（泛指“任何人”），与“都”“不”构成全称否定。
② 谁为主语、他为宾语（他话题化前置）：意为“谁都不认识他 / 没有一个人认识他”。其中“谁”同样是任指代词。
两句中的“谁”均为任指，既不是疑问用法，也不是虚指用法。
(b) 歧义根源：汉语代词无形态格标记（他/他同形，主宾无别），且允许话题前置与较灵活的词序；否定词“不”辖域在动词“认识”上，但主宾语法关系没有形态区分，使得“谁都”既可分析为“他”作主语小句的宾语，也可分析为“他”作宾语被前置的话题小句的主语。
(c) 不同。“他谁都认识”基本只有“他认识所有人”一种理解，“所有人都认识他”这一读法很难成立。原因：汉语句首位置强烈倾向解读为主语/话题，故句首“他”默认是主语；肯定全称“谁都认识”要把“他”当作宾语需依赖不被偏好、语用上较怪的话题化，而否定句里全称否定“谁都不”自身就可作自然的小句主语，因此“没人不认识他”的读法完全可得。故否定句歧义真实且强，肯定句基本无歧义。

## H4

【思考】
Let K = knight (always tells the truth), N = knave (always lies). The three statements are:
- A: "B is a knave."
- B: "A and C are of the same type."
- C: "A is a knight."

I test the two possibilities for A and show exactly one is consistent.

Assume A is a knight (K). Then A's statement is true, so B is a knave (N). Because B is a knave, B's statement is false. B said "A and C are of the same type"; for this to be false, A and C must be of DIFFERENT types. Since A = K, we get C = N. Now C is a knave, so C's statement must be false. C said "A is a knight." But A is in fact a knight, so C's statement is TRUE. This contradicts C being a knave. Therefore the assumption "A is a knight" is impossible; A must be a knave.

So A = N. Then A's statement "B is a knave" is false, which means B is NOT a knave, so B = K. Because B is a knight, B's statement is true: "A and C are of the same type." Since A = N, C must also be N. Now verify C = N: C said "A is a knight," but A is a knave, so that statement is false, consistent with C being a knave. All three statements are satisfied.

Thus the unique assignment is A = knave, B = knight, C = knave. I have excluded the alternative (A = knight) by deriving a contradiction, so no other assignment works.

【答案】
A 是无赖，B 是骑士，C 是无赖。
推理：先假设 A 是骑士，则“B 是无赖”为真→B 是无赖；B 说谎，故“A 与 C 同类”为假→A、C 不同类，因 A 是骑士，故 C 是无赖；但 C 作为无赖必须说谎，而“A 是骑士”实为真话，矛盾。因此 A 不可能为骑士，必为无赖。A 是无赖→“B 是无赖”为假→B 是骑士；B 说真话→“A 与 C 同类”为真，A 是无赖故 C 也是无赖；C 说“A 是骑士”为假，与其无赖身份一致。唯一解成立。

## H3

【思考】
Compare (A) "He didn't reply." and (B) "He didn't bother to reply."

(a) Literal propositional content: Both sentences assert exactly the same truth-conditional proposition — that he did not reply. There is no difference in what the world must be like for either to be true. The difference is in speaker stance/attitude: (A) is neutral report; (B) adds a subjective, evaluative coloring — the speaker conveys that replying would have been easy/cheap and that he ought to have replied, expressing irritation, criticism, or the judgment that he was lazy or inconsiderate.

(b) With "bother to":
- Presupposition: "bother to V" presupposes that V was an action within easy reach (minimal effort) and that there was some reasonable expectation or occasion calling for V. So (B) presupposes that replying was expected/called for and not difficult.
- Implicature (conversational): via the choice of "bother", the speaker implicates criticism — that he could easily have replied but chose not to, and that this was rude or lazy. This is a pragmatic implicature, cancelable in special contexts, not part of the literal truth conditions.

(c) Why (B) cannot describe something the speaker thinks "shouldn't be expected to happen": "didn't bother to V" carries a presupposition that V was owed/expected and easily doable; if no such expectation exists, using it is infelicitous (presupposition failure). Example: if you write to a random celebrity who has no reason to answer, you would NOT say "He didn't bother to reply" — that would falsely imply he had an obligation or was rude. Or "The stone didn't bother to reply" is absurd because a stone can never be expected to reply. So the phrase requires a default expectation of the action; without it, the sentence is inappropriate.

【答案】
(a) 字面命题内容无差别：两句真值条件相同，都断言“他没有回复”。差别在说话人立场：(A) 为中性陈述；(B) 加入主观评价，隐含“回复轻而易举、他本该回复”，传达不满、批评或认为他懒惰/失礼。
(b) 预设（presuppose）：回复本是情理之中、不难做到且被期待发生的动作。隐含（implicate）：说话人认为他本可轻松回复却故意不回，含批评/指责意味（这是可取消的会话含义，不进入真值条件）。
(c) “didn't bother to”预设该动作本被期待且轻而易举；若说话人认为某事本就不该被期待发生，使用此句会造成预设失败，故不成立。例：给一位毫无理由回信的陌生名人写信，不能说“He didn't bother to reply”，那会错误暗示对方有义务或失礼；又如“The stone didn't bother to reply”荒诞，因为石头不可能被期待回复。

## H2

【思考】
We form 7-digit numbers using each of {1,2,3,4,5,6,7} exactly once, and count those divisible by 11.

Divisibility by 11: a number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11. For digits d1 d2 d3 d4 d5 d6 d7 (d1 most significant), this is (d1 − d2 + d3 − d4 + d5 − d6 + d7) ≡ 0 (mod 11). Equivalently, let S_odd = sum of digits in odd positions {1,3,5,7} (4 digits) and S_even = sum in even positions {2,4,6} (3 digits). Then S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).

Total sum of all digits = 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28, so S_odd + S_even = 28.
Then S_odd − S_even = (S_odd + S_even) − 2·S_even = 28 − 2·S_even, which is even. Possible multiples of 11 in range: 0, ±11, ±22.
- If S_odd − S_even = 11 → 2·S_odd = 39 → S_odd = 19.5 (impossible).
- If = −11 → 2·S_odd = 17 → 8.5 (impossible).
- If = 22 → 2·S_odd = 50 → 25 (out of range; max S_odd = 4+5+6+7 = 22).
- If = −22 → 2·S_odd = 6 → 3 (below min S_odd = 1+2+3+4 = 10).
- If = 0 → S_odd = S_even = 14. This works.

So the necessary and sufficient condition is S_odd = 14 (which forces S_even = 14).

Now find all 4-element subsets of {1..7} summing to 14. Enumerating:
- {1,2,4,7} = 14
- {1,2,5,6} = 14
- {1,3,4,6} = 14
- {2,3,4,5} = 14
No others (verified: any subset not containing 1 needs ≥ 2+3+4+5 = 14 giving exactly {2,3,4,5}; subsets containing 1 were exhausted above). So there are 4 valid choices for the 4 odd-position digits; the remaining 3 digits automatically fill the even positions summing to 14.

For each choice: the 4 odd-position digits can be arranged in 4! = 24 ways, and the 3 even-position digits in 3! = 6 ways. So per choice: 24 × 6 = 144 numbers. Total = 4 × 144 = 576.

【答案】
共有 576 个。
推导：被 11 整除的判别法为奇数位数字和与偶数位数字和之差是 11 的倍数。七位数各位数字总和 1+2+3+4+5+6+7=28；设奇数位（4 位）和为 S_odd、偶数位（3 位）和为 S_even，则 S_odd+S_even=28，且 S_odd−S_even≡0 (mod 11)。逐一检验倍数：±11、±22 均导致 S_odd 非整数或越界，只有差为 0 可行，即 S_odd=S_even=14。从 1–7 中取 4 个数和为 14 的子集恰有 4 个：{1,2,4,7}、{1,2,5,6}、{1,3,4,6}、{2,3,4,5}。每个子集的 4 个数排在奇数位（4! = 24 种），余下 3 个数排在偶数位（3! = 6 种），故每个子集得 24×6=144 个数。总数 = 4×144 = 576。

## H1

【思考】
The line "王顾左右而言他" is from 《孟子·梁惠王下》. In context, 孟子 pressed the king with uncomfortable political questions; the king, unable to answer, turned to his attendants and spoke of other matters.

(a) Meaning of 他: In this pre-Qin context 他 means "other (things/matters)" — i.e., the king spoke of "something else / other topics," deflecting the conversation.

(b) Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "he/him": In pre-Qin Chinese, 他 was not used as a third-person pronoun. The third-person pronoun functions were served by 之, 其, 厥, etc. The word 他 originally meant "other / different" (as in 其他 = "other ones"); its use as a personal pronoun "he/him" developed much later (becoming common only in the vernacular/later periods). So 言他 = "speak of other things," not "speak of him."

(c) Concrete偏差 if read as third-person "he/him": The sentence would become "the king looked around at his attendants and spoke of HIM," implying the king started talking about some specific third-party male person. This loses the actual point — the king's evasion/deflection to unrelated topics to avoid 孟子's embarrassing questions — and is contextually absurd because there is no antecedent male "he" to refer to. Grammatically, 他 parallels 左右 (also an object of 言): 顾左右 "look at the attendants" and 言他 "speak of other matters" are parallel VP-object structures, both objects of the king's actions. Historical evidence: in 先秦, 他 = "other," e.g. 《诗经》"他山之石，可以攻玉" (other mountain's stone).

【答案】
(a) “他”意为“别的事/其他话题”，指大王转而去说不相干的事情以岔开话题。
(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词“他（他/她/它）”，因为在先秦汉语中“他”并无第三人称代词用法；当时第三人称由“之、其、厥”等承担。“他”本义为“别的、其他的”（如“其他”），作人称代词是后世（近代汉语/白话）才发展出来的。故“言他”是“说别的事”。
(c) 若按第三人称“他（某人）”理解，整句会变成“大王环顾左右而说起‘他’（某个男人）”，即大王转而谈论某个特定的第三者。这既在上下文中荒诞（前文并无可指代的男性第三者），又彻底丢失了原意——大王以岔开话题来回避孟子逼问的“顾左右而言他”之“避而不答”核心。句法上“他”与“左右”平行，同作“顾/言”的宾语（顾左右、言他），足证“他”是“别的事”而非代词。训诂佐证：先秦“他”皆表“其他”，如《诗经·小雅》“他山之石，可以攻玉”。
