## H6

【思考】
I will examine each sentence for collocational and grammatical correctness in standard English.

(1) "I'm looking forward to meet you." The phrase "look forward to" is a phrasal expression in which "to" is a preposition, not an infinitive marker. A preposition must be followed by a gerund (verb-ing form) or a noun phrase. The bare infinitive "meet" is ungrammatical after a preposition. Correction: "I'm looking forward to meeting you." Error type: preposition followed by a bare infinitive instead of a gerund.

(2) "She suggested me to take the train." The verb "suggest" does not license the pattern "suggest + indirect object + to-infinitive." The grammatical complement patterns are "suggest doing something" or "suggest (that) someone (should) do something" (a declarative/subjunctive clause). One cannot say "suggest someone to do." Correction: "She suggested (that) I take the train" or "She suggested taking the train." If the recipient must be made explicit, one may say "She suggested to me that I take the train," but never "suggested me to." Error type: invalid verb complementation (wrong "suggest + object + to-infinitive" pattern).

(3) "Despite of the rain, we went out." "Despite" is itself a preposition and is never followed by "of." The correct alternative pair is "in spite of." Therefore either drop "of" after "despite" or replace "despite" with "in spite of." Correction: "Despite the rain, we went out" or "In spite of the rain, we went out." Error type: redundant "of" after "despite."

(4) "He's capable to do it." The adjective "capable" is followed by the preposition "of" plus a gerund, never by a "to"-infinitive. Correction: "He's capable of doing it." Error type: wrong complementation (capable + of + gerund, not capable + to-infinitive).

(5) "I would rather stay than to go." The correlative construction "would rather ... than ..." takes bare infinitives on both sides; no "to" may appear after "than." Correction: "I would rather stay than go." Error type: extra "to" after "than" (should be a bare infinitive).

(6) "It's worth to try." The adjective "worth" is followed by a gerund (or by a noun phrase), never by a "to"-infinitive. Correction: "It's worth trying" or "It's worth a try." Error type: wrong complementation (worth + gerund, not worth + to-infinitive).

【答案】
(1) 错误。look forward to 中的 to 是介词，后接动名词而非不定式。改为：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误。suggest 不能接"宾语 + to do"结构，应说 suggest (that) sb (should) do 或 suggest doing。改为：She suggested (that) I take the train. 或 She suggested taking the train.
(3) 错误。despite 是介词，后面不能加 of；应删去 of（或改用 in spite of）。改为：Despite the rain, we went out. 或 In spite of the rain, we went out.
(4) 错误。capable 后接 of + 动名词，不接 to do。改为：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误。would rather ... than ... 两端都用动词原形，than 后不应有 to。改为：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误。worth 后接动名词或名词，不接 to do。改为：It's worth trying. 或 It's worth a try.

## H5

【思考】
I analyze the sentence "他谁都不认识."

First, identify the possible readings. Chinese word order alone does not mark morphological case, so "他" can be either the subject or a fronted object (topic). The phrase "谁都" is a universally quantified phrase meaning "everyone / anyone" (the "都" marks totality). With the negation "不," the sentence admits two distinct structural parses:

Reading 1 (他 = subject, 谁 = object): "他 [对] 谁都不认识" = He does not know anyone / He knows nobody. Here "谁" is 任指 (arbitrary / free reference), referring to any person within the domain; in a negative sentence it yields the meaning "no person."

Reading 2 (他 = fronted object / topic, 谁 = subject): "（关于）他，谁都不认识（他）" = No one knows him / Everyone does not know him. Here "他" is the object that has been topicalized to the front, and "谁" functions as the subject (also 任指).

Thus the ambiguity is a grammatical-relation and scope ambiguity: "他" may be the subject who does not know, or the object who is not known.

(b) Root of the ambiguity: (i) Chinese lacks morphological case marking, so a noun phrase such as "他" can serve as either subject or object depending on interpretation; (ii) object preposing / topicalization is grammatical, allowing "他" to be a preposed object while "谁都" remains the subject; (iii) the universal quantifier "谁都" can take scope over the subject position, and the negation "不" can scope over the whole clause, producing two distinct logical structures: ¬(他 认识 谁) versus (谁 都 不 认识 他).

(c) Compare with "他谁都认识" (He knows everyone / Everyone knows him). Technically both sentences are ambiguous (each permits the two readings). However, the degree and salience of the ambiguity differ. The affirmative "他谁都认识" is strongly biased toward Reading 1 ("He knows everyone," 他 = subject), and its Reading 2 ("Everyone knows him") is much weaker and more marginal. By contrast, the negative "他谁都不认识" has a more robust second reading ("Nobody knows him"), which is quite readily available. The difference arises because negation naturally scopes over the universal quantifier and licenses a "no one" interpretation in which "谁" is the subject of a negative proposition; the affirmative lacks this pressure, and the fronted "他" more strongly retains subjecthood. Also, information structure and the non-symmetric agency of "认识" make the object-topicalization reading more marked in the affirmative case.

【答案】
(a) 两种理解：
- 理解一：他是主语、"谁"（任指，意为"任何人"）是宾语 → "他什么都不认识"，即"他不认识任何人／他谁都不认识"。此处"谁"为任指用法。
- 理解二：他是前置话题/宾语，"谁都"作主语 → "谁都不认识他"，即"没有人认识他"。此处"谁"同样是任指（作主语）。
(b) 歧义根源于句法与形态：汉语无格标记，"他"可作主、宾语；宾语前置（话题化）合法，使"他"可以是前置宾语而"谁都"留在主语位；同时全称量词"谁都"可与否定"不"搭配生成两种逻辑结构——¬(他认识谁) 与 (谁都不认识他)，造成主语/宾语关系与量化辖域的歧义。
(c) "他谁都认识" 同样有两种理解（"他认识所有人"与"所有人都认识他"），但歧义程度不同：肯定句强烈偏向"他认识所有人"（他是主语），"所有人都认识他"这一读解很弱、边缘化；而否定句"他谁都不认识"的"没有人认识他"读解相当自然、明显。差别在于：否定词"不"自然辖域于全称量化之上，容易生成"谁都不（认识他）"的命题；肯定句缺少这种推力，"他"更稳固地保持主语身份。

## H4

【思考】
We have three people A, B, C; each is either a Knight (always tells the truth) or a Knave (always lies). Their statements are:
- A: "B is a knave."
- B: "A and C are of the same type."
- C: "A is a knight."

I perform a complete case analysis on A's identity, since A must be either a Knight or a Knave.

Case 1: Suppose A is a Knight. Then A's statement is true, so B is a Knave. Since B is a Knave, B's statement must be false. B said "A and C are of the same type." For this to be false, A and C must be of different types. We have A = Knight, so C must be Knave. Now check C's statement: C is a Knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false, which would mean A is not a knight (A is a Knave). But this contradicts our assumption that A is a Knight. Therefore Case 1 is impossible.

Case 2: Suppose A is a Knave. Then A's statement "B is a knave" is false, so B is a Knight. Since B is a Knight, B's statement is true: "A and C are of the same type." We have A = Knave, so C must also be Knave. Now check C's statement: C is a Knave, so "A is a knight" must be false → A is not a knight → A is a Knave. This is consistent with our assumption. 

Thus the only consistent assignment is: A = Knave, B = Knight, C = Knave.

To be thorough, note that we have exhausted all possibilities for A (the only two options). The assignment where A is a Knight leads to a contradiction and is therefore excluded. Hence the solution is unique.

【答案】
A 是无赖，B 是骑士，C 是无赖。

推理：先假设 A 是骑士，则 A 说真话 → B 是无赖；B 说谎 → "A 与 C 同类"为假 → A、C 不同类，因 A 是骑士，故 C 是无赖；但 C 是无赖则其话"A 是骑士"为假 → A 不是骑士，矛盾。故 A 不可能是骑士。于是 A 必为无赖 → A 说谎 → "B 是无赖"为假 → B 是骑士；B 说真话 → "A 与 C 同类"为真，A 是无赖故 C 也是无赖；C 是无赖则其话"A 是骑士"为假 → A 不是骑士，与 A 是无赖一致。故唯一解：A 无赖、B 骑士、C 无赖。

## H3

【思考】
Compare (A) "He didn't reply." and (B) "He didn't bother to reply."

(a) Literal propositional content: Both sentences assert exactly the same proposition — that he did not produce a reply. There is no difference in truth-conditional, propositional meaning. The difference lies in the speaker's stance / attitude. Sentence (A) is neutral and merely reports the absence of a reply. Sentence (B) adds a subjective, evaluative layer: by using "bother to," the speaker frames the non-reply as a deliberate refusal to expend a trivial amount of effort, implying carelessness, laziness, or dismissiveness on the part of "him."

(b) Presupposition of "bother to": The construction presupposes that replying was a feasible, low-cost action within his capacity and that a reply was at least minimally expected or warranted (a small courtesy). Implicature (conversational): The speaker conveys that he was inconsiderate or lazy — that he could easily have replied but chose not to make even that small effort. The use of "bother" signals the speaker's negative evaluation of his conduct.

(c) Why it cannot describe something the speaker thinks "should not have been expected": "bother to V" presupposes that V-ing is a trivial, expectable action and that not doing it constitutes a fault / omission by the agent. If the speaker believes the action was not at all expected (e.g., replying to a scam / spam email, or a complete stranger responding), then describing the non-performance as "didn't bother" is infelicitous — it wrongly presupposes the reply was warranted and criticizes the agent for a non-omission. Example: "He didn't bother to reply to the scam email" sounds odd, because one would not expect (and should not want) a reply to a scam; saying "didn't bother" incorrectly implies he should have replied and was negligent. Similarly, "The baby didn't bother to thank me" is absurd, because a baby cannot be expected to thank. Thus the expression requires a genuine, reasonable expectation of the action.

【答案】
(a) 两句话的字面命题内容完全相同，都断言"他没有回复"这一事实，没有真值条件上的差别。差别在说话人立场：(A) 中性陈述"他没回复"；(B) 通过 bother to 加入主观评价，暗示"他本可轻易回复却懒得／不愿费这点力气"，带有对其粗心、怠慢、不以为然的批评意味。
(b) 预设（presuppose）：回复是一件他力所能及、代价极小、且至少应被最低限度期待／合情理的小举动。隐含（implicate）：他为人敷衍、懒散、不把对方放在心上——本可顺手回复却连这点努力都不肯付出，说话人对其行为持负面态度。
(c) 因为 bother to 预设该动作是"微不足道且本应被期待"的，否定式则把"未做"刻画为施事可指责的疏失。若说话人认为此事"本就不该被期待发生"（即根本没有理由期待），此预设即被违反，句子不成立。例：说 "He didn't bother to reply to the scam email" 很别扭，因为没人会期待（也不该期待）回复诈骗邮件；用 didn't bother 反而错误暗示他"本该回复"且失职。又如 "The baby didn't bother to thank me" 荒谬，因为婴儿根本不可能被期待致谢。

## H2

【思考】
We must count the 7-digit numbers formed by using each of the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 exactly once, that are divisible by 11.

Divisibility rule for 11: A number is divisible by 11 if and only if the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11, i.e., (sum of digits in odd positions) − (sum of digits in even positions) ≡ 0 (mod 11).

For a 7-digit number with positions 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (from left to right): odd positions are 1, 3, 5, 7 (four positions); even positions are 2, 4, 6 (three positions). Let S_odd = sum of digits in odd positions, S_even = sum of digits in even positions.

Total sum of all digits = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 = 28. So S_odd + S_even = 28.

Divisibility condition: S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).
Substitute S_even = 28 − S_odd: S_odd − (28 − S_odd) = 2·S_odd − 28 ≡ 0 (mod 11).
Thus 2·S_odd ≡ 28 (mod 11). Since 28 ≡ 6 (mod 11), we need 2·S_odd ≡ 6 (mod 11), which gives S_odd ≡ 3 (mod 11).

Now S_odd is the sum of 4 distinct digits chosen from {1,2,3,4,5,6,7}. Minimum possible: 1+2+3+4 = 10. Maximum possible: 4+5+6+7 = 22. So S_odd ∈ [10, 22]. The only value in this range congruent to 3 mod 11 is 14 (since 3 is below the range, 14 = 3+11, and 25 > 22). Therefore S_odd must equal 14, and consequently S_even = 28 − 14 = 14.

So we need to choose 4 distinct digits from {1..7} that sum to 14; these occupy the odd positions, and the remaining 3 digits (which then also sum to 14) occupy the even positions.

Enumerate all 4-element subsets of {1,2,3,4,5,6,7} summing to 14:
- {1, 2, 4, 7} (sum = 14)
- {1, 2, 5, 6} (sum = 14)
- {1, 3, 4, 6} (sum = 14)
- {2, 3, 4, 5} (sum = 14)

Check complements: {3,5,6}, {3,4,7}, {2,5,7}, {1,6,7} — each sums to 14, confirming consistency. No other 4-subsets sum to 14. So there are exactly 4 valid digit groupings.

For each such grouping:
- Arrange the 4 chosen digits in the 4 odd positions: 4! = 24 ways.
- Arrange the 3 complement digits in the 3 even positions: 3! = 6 ways.
- Total per grouping: 24 × 6 = 144.

With 4 groupings: 4 × 144 = 576.

Therefore, the number of 7-digit numbers divisible by 11 is 576.

【答案】
共有 576 个。

推导：被 11 整除的判别法是奇数位数字和与偶数位数字和之差为 11 的倍数（含 0）。七位数奇数位（1,3,5,7）共 4 位、偶数位（2,4,6）共 3 位。1~7 总和为 28。设奇数位和为 S_奇，则 S_奇 + S_偶 = 28，且 S_奇 − S_偶 ≡ 0 (mod 11) → 2S_奇 − 28 ≡ 0 (mod 11) → S_奇 ≡ 3 (mod 11)。S_奇 为 4 个不同数字之和，范围 10~22，其中唯一 ≡ 3 (mod 11) 的值是 14，故 S_奇 = S_偶 = 14。从 {1..7} 取 4 个不同数和为 14 的子集有：{1,2,4,7}、{1,2,5,6}、{1,3,4,6}、{2,3,4,5} 共 4 组。每组中 4 个数排在 4 个奇数位有 4! = 24 种，剩余 3 个数排在 3 个偶数位有 3! = 6 种，每组 24×6 = 144 个；共 4×144 = 576 个。

## H1

【思考】
The sentence "王顾左右而言他" appears in Mencius (《孟子·梁惠王下》).

(a) Meaning of "他": In this context, "他" means "其他的事 / 别的事" (other things, something else). The king looked to the left and right and spoke of other matters — that is, he changed the subject to avoid answering. So "他" here is "别的、其他的（事情）," not a person.

(b) Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "他" (he/him): In pre-Qin Chinese, "他" was not used as a third-person personal pronoun. The third-person pronoun "他" (referring to a male person) developed much later, becoming common only around the Tang dynasty / medieval period. In classical Chinese, "他" (often written 他 / 佗 / 它) meant "别的、其他的" (other, another). Moreover, grammatically, "言他" means "言（于）他" = speak of other things, where "他" is the object meaning "other matters," not a pronoun referring to a person. The genuine pre-Qin third-person references were expressed by "之" (object) and "其" (genitive / subject), not "他."

(c) Concretely, if one mistakenly reads "他" as the modern third-person pronoun "him/he," the sentence would become "The king looked left and right and spoke to / about him," implying the king turned and addressed some third male person. This destroys the actual meaning and the rhetorical point: Mencius uses this to show the king evading a difficult question by diverting to unrelated topics. A pronoun reading also requires an antecedent (who is "him"?), which the context does not supply, rendering the sentence incoherent. Thus the pre-Qin usage of "他" as "other" must be respected.

【答案】
(a) "他"意为"别的、其他的（事情）"，即"王看了看左右，说起别的事情（岔开话题）"。
(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词"他"，因为先秦汉语中"他"不作第三人称代词（第三人称代词"他"是后世——约唐代以后——才发展出来的用法）。先秦"他"（亦作"佗""它"）的意思是"别的、其他的"。且句法上"言他"即"言（于）他"——言说别的事，"他"是宾语，表"其他事物"而非指人代词；当时第三人称用"之"（宾语）、"其"（定语/主语）。
(c) 若按第三人称代词理解，整句会变成"王环顾左右，对他（某人）说话"，把"他"当成某个男性第三者的宾语/指代。这完全偏离原意：原句是说王用岔开话题来回避难题（孟子以此说明王被问住而顾左右言他），修辞意图尽失；同时代词读法需要一个先行词（"他"指谁？），而语境中并无此指代对象，句子会变得不通。故必须按先秦"他＝别的/其他的"来理解。
