# 高难度测试卷作答（顺序：H3, H1, H5, H2, H6, H4）

## H3

【思考】
Step 1: Identify the literal propositional content of (A) "He didn't reply" and (B) "He didn't bother to reply." Both sentences assert the same basic proposition: the referent (he) did not perform the act of replying. At the level of truth-conditional content, the two sentences are identical: in any world where he failed to reply, both are true; where he replied, both are false.

Step 2: Consider speaker stance. Sentence (A) is neutral: it merely reports an absence of a reply. Sentence (B) adds the phrase "bother to," which imports an evaluative stance. "Bother" encodes that the action would have required only a small, trivial amount of effort, and that the agent chose not to expend even that. Therefore (B) conveys that the speaker judges the non-reply as a sign of laziness, disregard, or rudeness, and that a reply was expected or owed.

Step 3: Presupposition of "bother to." The use of "bother to V" presupposes (i) that V was an available, low-cost option for the agent (the agent had the capacity and opportunity to do V), and (ii) that performing V would have been a minor effort. So "He didn't bother to reply" presupposes that replying was easily within his power and would have cost little.

Step 4: Implicature of "bother to." By asserting that he did not expend even trivial effort, the speaker conversationally implicates (via Grice's maxims, especially relation and manner) that a reply was normatively expected — that he should have replied, and that his failure reflects a fault (carelessness or deliberate slight). The implicature is cancellable but default.

Step 5: Why it cannot describe something not expected. "Bother to" inherently presupposes a small, easily-performed action that the agent could have done but declined, thereby expressing criticism for violating a norm of minimal effort. If the speaker believes the action was "not to be expected anyway," there is no norm to violate and no trivial effort declined, so "bother to" is infelicitous. Example: "The baby didn't bother to reply to my question." A baby cannot reply at all, so there is no small effort he declined; the sentence absurdly implies the baby was lazily refusing. Another example: "He didn't bother to reply to the letter I never sent him." Since no letter was sent, no reply was expected, so "bother to" misattributes a normative expectation.

【答案】
(a) 两句话的字面命题内容相同：都断言"他没有回复"。差别在说话人立场：(A)是中性陈述，(B)通过 "bother to" 表达评价立场，暗示回复本是一件轻而易举、且本应/可期待发生的小事，而对方连这点力气都不愿花，带有批评、轻慢或不屑的意味。
(b) 预设（presuppose）：回复对他是唾手可得、只需极小代价就能做到的事（他有能力、有机会回复）。隐含（implicate，会话含义）：说话人认为他本应当回复、也期待他会回复，其不回复反映出懒惰、怠慢或故意冷落。
(c) "bother to" 本身预设该动作是代理人"本可轻易做到却选择不做"的小事，并据此表达"未达最低努力标准"的批评。若说话人认为此事"本就不该期待发生"，就不存在被违反的规范，也不存在"被放弃的微小努力"，用语便不恰当。例："那个婴儿根本没费心回复我的问题"——婴儿根本不会说话，谈不上"不愿花力气"，此句荒谬；又如"他没费心回复那封我根本没寄出的信"——信都没寄，本无回复期待，"bother to" 错误植入了规范期待。

## H1

【思考】
Step 1: Locate the source. The sentence "王顾左右而言他" appears in Mencius (《孟子·梁惠王下》), in a dialogue where Mencius presses the king with a series of escalating questions about a ruler's responsibility, and the king, unable to answer, turns aside.
Step 2: Determine the meaning of "他" in this context. In pre-Qin Chinese, "他" (often written 它/佗) meant "other, another, different" — not a third-person pronoun. So "言他" means "speak of other things / change the subject," and the whole sentence means "The king looked to his left and right and spoke of other matters (to evade the question)."
Step 3: Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "he/him." In classical (pre-Qin) Chinese, third-person reference was expressed by "之" (object) and "其" (possessive/subject modifier), not by "他." The use of "他" as a third-person personal pronoun developed much later, becoming common only in medieval/vernacular Chinese (roughly Tang–Song period and after, fully established in the modern vernacular). Therefore reading "他" as "him" is anachronistic and grammatically impossible in the Mencius text.
Step 4: The concrete deviation if read as a pronoun. If "他" were "him," "王顾左右而言他" would mean "The king looked left and right and spoke about him (some third person)." This changes the meaning from an evasive change of subject ("the king dodged the question by talking about something else") to a statement that the king mentioned a specific other person. The entire pragmatic point — the king's embarrassed, deliberate deflection — is lost, and the famous idiom (meaning "to evade a question by changing the subject") becomes unintelligible.

【答案】
(a) "他"在这里是"别的、其他的"之意；"言他"即"说别的事/转移话题"。全句意为：大王环顾左右，说起别的事情（以回避问题）。
(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词"他"，因为在先秦汉语中，第三人称代词用"之"（宾语）、"其"（定语/主语修饰）等，而不用"他"。"他"表示第三人称代词的用法是后世（约唐宋以后、直至近代白话）才逐渐产生的。所以把《孟子》里的"他"当作人称代词，是时代错置，在原文语法中不成立。
(c) 若按"第三人称代词"理解，整句会变成"大王环顾左右，说起他（某个第三人）"——意思是大王提到了某个特定的人。这与原意"大王用说别的事来回避问题"完全偏差，原句所表达的"顾左右而言他"这一"借题发挥、转移话题以规避诘问"的语用意图就荡然无存，成语义也无法成立。

## H5

【思考】
Step 1: Parse the sentence "他谁都不认识." It contains two nominal elements, "他" and the universal-indefinite "谁都," and the transitive verb "认识" (to know). Chinese has no case marking and flexible word order, so "他" can be either the subject or the object, and "谁都" can be either the subject (a universal quantifier meaning "everyone/anyone") or the object.
Step 2: Reading 1 — "他" as subject. "他 [subject] 谁都 [object, universal] 不认识" = "He knows no one / He doesn't know anyone." Here "谁" is 任指 (free/total reference), meaning "anyone/whoever," and with 都 + negation it forms a negative universal over the object.
Step 3: Reading 2 — "他" as object. "谁都 [subject, universal] 不认识 他 [object]" = "No one knows him." On the surface the object "他" is fronted (topicalized): "他，谁都不认识" = "Him, no one knows." Here "谁" is again 任指, functioning as the universal subject.
Step 4: In both readings "谁" is 任指, not interrogative (疑问) and not vague/imaginary reference (虚指). It is bound by 都 and the negation to express a universal quantity.
Step 5: Root of ambiguity. The ambiguity is syntactic: Chinese lacks explicit subject/object case markers and allows object fronting (topic promotion) as well as flexible argument order. The transitive verb "认识" has two arguments; with no morphological or positional disambiguation, either "他" or "谁都" can occupy the subject slot, yielding two distinct but both grammatical parses.
Step 6: Compare with "他谁都认识." This positive counterpart is also two-way ambiguous: (i) "He knows everyone" (他 subject) and (ii) "Everyone knows him" (他 object, fronted). Structurally the ambiguity is of the same type and same degree — both admit the subject/object reversal. However, there is a subtle pragmatic asymmetry: in the negative sentence, the phrase "谁都不认识" readily fossilizes as an independent negative-universal subject "nobody (knows)," so the "Nobody knows him" reading is somewhat more accessible than the corresponding "Everyone knows him" reading is in the positive sentence, where "都" more naturally modifies the object ("knows everyone"). But strictly, both sentences exhibit the identical two-way structural ambiguity.

【答案】
(a) 两种理解：
  1) "他"(主语) 谁都(任指宾语) 不认识 → "他谁都不认识 = 他不认识任何人 / 他谁也不认识。" 这里"谁"是任指（表示"任何人/无论谁"），与"都""不"配合构成对宾语的周遍性否定。
  2) "谁都"(任指主语) 不认识 他(宾语) → "没人认识他。" 表层是宾语"他"话题化前置："他，谁都不认识。" 这里"谁"同样是任指，作周遍性主语。
  两种理解中"谁"都是任指，不是疑问，也不是虚指。
(b) 歧义的句法/形态根源：汉语没有主宾格标记，且允许宾语话题化前置、论元语序灵活。"认识"是二元及物动词，两个论元"他"与"谁都"均无形态区分，谁作主语、谁作宾语均可，于是产生两种都合语法的分析。
(c) "他谁都认识"同样具有双向歧义："他认识所有人"（他为主语）与"所有人都认识他"（他为前置宾语）。就歧义类型而言二者相同，程度也基本相当——都允许主宾语互换的两种解读。细微差别在于：否定句"谁都不认识"更易凝结为否定周遍主语"没人（认识）"，因此"没人认识他"这一解读比肯定句"所有人都认识他"解读更显自然/更易被触发；但严格来说，两句都具备完全相同的双向结构歧义。

## H2

【思考】
Step 1: Divisibility by 11 rule. A number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11. For a 7-digit number with digits d1 d2 d3 d4 d5 d6 d7, compute (d1+d3+d5+d7) − (d2+d4+d6); it must be ≡ 0 (mod 11).
Step 2: Total digit sum. The digits are 1,2,3,4,5,6,7, each used once. Their sum is 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28.
Step 3: Define S_odd = sum of the 4 digits in odd positions (1,3,5,7), S_even = sum of the 3 digits in even positions (2,4,6). Then S_odd + S_even = 28, and the divisibility condition is S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).
Step 4: Express the difference. S_odd − S_even = S_odd − (28 − S_odd) = 2·S_odd − 28. Need 2·S_odd − 28 ≡ 0 (mod 11). Since 28 ≡ 6 (mod 11), this is 2·S_odd ≡ 6 (mod 11). Multiply both sides by the inverse of 2 modulo 11, which is 6 (because 2·6 = 12 ≡ 1). So S_odd ≡ 6·6 = 36 ≡ 3 (mod 11).
Step 5: Find feasible S_odd. S_odd is the sum of 4 distinct digits from {1..7}; its minimum is 1+2+3+4 = 10 and maximum is 4+5+6+7 = 22. The only value in [10,22] congruent to 3 mod 11 is 14 (since 3 is too small and 25 too large). Check directly: S_odd = 14 gives difference 2·14 − 28 = 0, which is divisible by 11. Other multiples (±11, ±22) give non-integer or out-of-range S_odd, so S_odd = 14 is the unique solution.
Step 6: Count the 4-digit subsets summing to 14. Equivalently, the 3 even-position digits sum to 28 − 14 = 14. Enumerate 3-element subsets of {1..7} summing to 14: {1,6,7}, {2,5,7}, {3,4,7}, {3,5,6}. That is 4 subsets. Their complements (the 4 odd-position digits) are {2,3,4,5}, {1,3,4,6}, {1,2,5,6}, {1,2,4,7}, each summing to 14. So there are 4 ways to choose which digits occupy the odd positions.
Step 7: Arrange. For each choice, the 4 odd-position digits can be permuted in 4! = 24 ways, and the 3 even-position digits in 3! = 6 ways. All digits are nonzero, so no leading-zero problem. Total = 4 × 24 × 6 = 576.

【答案】
能被 11 整除的七位数共有 576 个。
推导：被 11 整除的判别法是奇数位数字和与偶数位数字和之差为 11 的倍数。数字 1–7 总和为 28。设奇数位（4 位）数字和为 S_odd，偶数位（3 位）为 S_even，则 S_odd + S_even = 28，要求 S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11)。代入得 2·S_odd − 28 ≡ 0 (mod 11) → S_odd ≡ 3 (mod 11)。S_odd 为 4 个不同数字之和，范围 10–22，其中唯一 ≡3 (mod 11) 的是 14。故需选出 4 个数字和为 14（其余 3 个和亦为 14）。3 个数字和为 14 的组合有 {1,6,7}、{2,5,7}、{3,4,7}、{3,5,6} 共 4 种，其补集即奇数位选法。每种选法下，奇数位 4! 排列、偶数位 3! 排列，总计 4 × 24 × 6 = 576。

## H6

【思考】
Step 1: (1) "I'm looking forward to meet you." Error: "look forward to" takes a gerund because "to" is a preposition here, not the infinitive marker. Correct: "I'm looking forward to meeting you."
Step 2: (2) "She suggested me to take the train." Error: "suggest" is not a ditransitive verb and cannot take an object + infinitive ("suggest someone to do"). Correct forms: "She suggested (that) I take the train" or "She suggested taking the train." (If an object + infinitive is wanted, use "advise": "She advised me to take the train.")
Step 3: (3) "Despite of the rain, we went out." Error: "despite" is a preposition and is followed directly by a noun phrase; "despite of" is ungrammatical. Correct: "Despite the rain, we went out." (The "of" version is "in spite of the rain.")
Step 4: (4) "He's capable to do it." Error: "capable" is followed by "of + gerund," not an infinitive. Correct: "He's capable of doing it."
Step 5: (5) "I would rather stay than to go." Error: after "would rather ... than," the bare infinitive is required; "than to go" wrongly uses "to." Correct: "I would rather stay than go."
Step 6: (6) "It's worth to try." Error: "worth" is followed by a gerund or a noun, not an infinitive. Correct: "It's worth trying." (Or "It's worth a try.")

【答案】
(1) 错误（介词后接不定式）。"look forward to" 中 to 是介词，应接动名词。正确：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误（动词搭配）。suggest 不能接"宾语 + 不定式"(suggest sb to do)。正确：She suggested (that) I take the train. 或 She suggested taking the train.（若要"建议某人做某事"可用 advise：She advised me to take the train.）
(3) 错误（冗余 of）。despite 是介词，直接接名词短语，不能说 despite of。正确：Despite the rain, we went out.（或 In spite of the rain）
(4) 错误（形容词搭配）。capable 后接 of + 动名词，不接不定式。正确：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误（句型）。would rather ... than 后用动词原形（不带 to）。正确：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误（形容词搭配）。worth 后接动名词或名词，不接不定式。正确：It's worth trying.（或 It's worth a try.）

## H4

【思考】
Step 1: Set up. Each of A, B, C is a knight (always truthful) or a knave (always lies). Statements: A: "B is a knave." B: "A and C are of the same type." C: "A is a knight."
Step 2: Suppose A is a knight. Then A's statement is true, so B is a knave. Since B is a knave, B's statement is false; thus "A and C are the same type" is false, meaning A and C differ in type. A is a knight, so C must be a knave. But then C's statement "A is a knight" would be a true statement made by a knave, which is impossible. Contradiction. Therefore A cannot be a knight; A is a knave.
Step 3: Since A is a knave, A's statement "B is a knave" is false, so B is not a knave — B is a knight.
Step 4: Since B is a knight, B's statement is true: "A and C are the same type." A is a knave, so C must also be a knave.
Step 5: Verify C. C is a knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. A is indeed a knave, so the statement is false — consistent.
Step 6: Uniqueness/exclusion. The case analysis on A covers all possibilities (A is either knight or knave). A knight leads to contradiction; A knave deterministically forces B knight and C knave, which is fully consistent. Hence the unique solution is A = knave, B = knight, C = knave.

【答案】
A 是无赖，B 是骑士，C 是无赖。
推理：假设 A 是骑士，则 A 说" B 是无赖"为真，故 B 是无赖；B 是无赖则其话" A 和 C 同类"为假，即 A、C 不同类，A 是骑士故 C 是无赖；但 C 若为无赖，其话" A 是骑士"却是真的，矛盾。所以 A 必为无赖。A 是无赖，则" B 是无赖"为假，故 B 是骑士；B 是骑士则其话为真，A、C 同类，故 C 也是无赖；验证 C 的话" A 是骑士"为假（A 确为无赖），自洽。其他可能均被排除，唯一解为：A 无赖、B 骑士、C 无赖。
