## H6

【思考】
I will examine each of the six learner sentences and judge its grammaticality/naturalness against standard English collocation rules.

(1) "I'm looking forward to meet you."
The phrase "look forward to" contains the word "to" which here is a **preposition**, not an infinitive marker. Prepositions must be followed by a noun phrase or a gerund (verb-ing), never by a bare infinitive. Therefore "meet" is incorrect; it must be "meeting". Correct form: "I'm looking forward to meeting you."

(2) "She suggested me to take the train."
The verb "suggest" does not take the pattern "suggest + indirect object + to-infinitive". The accepted patterns are: (a) "suggest that someone (should) do something" (a that-clause with subjunctive/indicative), or (b) "suggest doing something". Saying "suggest me to..." is ungrammatical. A verb that does allow "me to" is "advise" or "recommend". Correct forms: "She suggested that I take the train" or "She suggested taking the train." (One could also say "She advised me to take the train.")

(3) "Despite of the rain, we went out."
"Despite" is itself a preposition and is never followed by "of". The correct preposition is simply "despite". The phrase "in spite of" does take "of", but "despite" does not. Correct: "Despite the rain, we went out." (Alternatively: "In spite of the rain, we went out.")

(4) "He's capable to do it."
The adjective "capable" is followed by the preposition "of" plus a gerund, not by a to-infinitive. Pattern: "capable of doing". Correct: "He's capable of doing it." (Contrast with "able to do", which does take the infinitive; "capable" and "able" are not interchangeable in their complementation.)

(5) "I would rather stay than to go."
The correlative "would rather ... than ..." requires bare infinitives (without "to") after both "rather" and "than". The "to" before "go" is superfluous and ungrammatical. Correct: "I would rather stay than go."

(6) "It's worth to try."
The adjective "worth" is followed by a gerund (-ing), never by a to-infinitive. Pattern: "worth doing". Correct: "It's worth trying."

【答案】
六句均不自然/不正确，错误类型与改正如下：
(1) 错误类型：介词后误用不定式。"look forward to" 中的 to 是介词，须接动名词。正确表达：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误类型：suggest 不能接"间接宾语 + to 不定式"。正确表达：She suggested that I take the train. 或 She suggested taking the train.（也可改用 She advised me to take the train.）
(3) 错误类型：冗余的 of。despite 本身是介词，不加 of（in spite of 才加 of）。正确表达：Despite the rain, we went out.
(4) 错误类型：形容词互补错误。capable 须接 of + 动名词，不接 to 不定式。正确表达：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误类型：冗余的 to。would rather ... than ... 两端都用原形不定式。正确表达：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误类型：worth 后误用不定式，应接动名词。正确表达：It's worth trying.

## H5

【思考】
The sentence "他谁都不认识" is structurally ambiguous. It contains: the third-person pronoun 他, the wh-origin word 谁, the universal adverb 都, and the negated verb 不认识 ("not know").

Reading 1 (他 as subject): 他 is the subject of 认识, and 谁 is the object bound by 都 within a negative context. Here 谁 functions as a 任指 (free/arbitrary reference) item meaning "anyone / anybody". The meaning is: "He does not know anyone" (for every person x, he does not know x). 

Reading 2 (他 as object): 谁 is the subject, and with 都 + 不 it forms a negative universal "nobody"; 他 is the object of 认识. The meaning is: "Nobody knows him" (for every person x, x does not know him). Here 谁 is again 任指, but inside the 谁…都…不 construction it yields a universal negative subject "nobody".

In both readings 谁 is 任指 (a universal/free-reference use), NOT a true interrogative (疑问) and NOT a vague/indefinite 虚指 (as in "谁来了" = "someone came").

Syntactic/morphological root of the ambiguity: Chinese is an isolating language with no morphological case marking, so 他 cannot be marked as nominative versus accusative. Together with the fact that the universal quantifier 都 can float and bind the wh-word 谁, and that 他 can appear pre-verbally whether it is subject or object, the sentence permits two scopings: either 他 takes scope as subject over the 谁…都 phrase, or the 谁…都 phrase takes scope as subject with 他 as object. The negation 不 distributes over the universal but does not itself decide the scope.

Comparison with "他谁都认识": This positive counterpart is also two-way ambiguous: "He knows everyone" (他 subject) and "Everyone knows him" (他 object). The ambiguity TYPE is the same. However, there is a pragmatic/processing asymmetry. In the positive sentence the 他-as-subject reading ("He knows everyone") is typically the primary, most natural reading, while the 他-as-object reading is secondary. In the negative sentence both readings remain available, but the 他-as-object reading ("Nobody knows him") is relatively more accessible because 谁…都不 naturally forms a negative universal subject ("nobody"), making it a plausible sentential subject. Thus the ambiguity is logically of the same kind, but the two readings do not compete with exactly equal strength: the positive form leans more strongly toward one reading, whereas the negative form distributes the two readings more evenly (and may even favor the object reading). So the ambiguity "degree" is not perfectly identical.

【答案】
(a) 两种理解：
- 理解一（他作主语）："他谁都不认识" = 他一个人都不认识（他不认识任何人）。句中"谁"是任指，相当于"任何人"，在"都+不"的否定句中表全称否定。
- 理解二（他作宾语）："谁都不认识他" = 谁也不认识他（没有人认识他）。句中"谁"仍是任指，"谁…都…不"构成否定全称主语"没有人"，"他"是"认识"的宾语。
两种理解里"谁"都是任指（全称用法），既不是疑问代词，也不是虚指（如"谁来了"表"有人来"那种虚指）。

(b) 歧义的根源：汉语是孤立语，名词/代词没有形态格标记，"他"无法区分主格与宾格；同时全称副词"都"可以浮动并约束疑问来源的"谁"，而"他"无论作主、宾语都能前置。于是"他"与"谁…都"之间出现两种辖域关系（他辖域大于谁→他主语；谁…都辖域大于他→他宾语），造成歧义。

(c) 与"他谁都认识"相比，歧义程度并不完全相同。后者同样有两解（"他认识所有人"／"所有人都认识他"），类型相同；但语用/加工上有不对称："他谁都认识"通常首选"他作主语"（他认识所有人）这一读解，另一读较弱；而"他谁都不认识"中"谁…都不"天然构成否定全称主语"没有人"，使"他作宾语"（没有人认识他）这一读解相对更易浮现，两读竞争更均衡（甚至偏向宾语句）。因此是同类歧义，但两读的凸显程度有差别。

## H4

【思考】
There are three people A, B, C. Each is either a Knight (always tells the truth) or a Knave (always lies). Their statements:
- A: "B is a knave."
- B: "A and C are of the same type."
- C: "A is a knight."

I will test the two possibilities for A and derive consequences, then verify against all statements.

Case 1: Suppose A is a Knight. Then A's statement is true, so "B is a knave" is true → B is a Knave. Since B is a Knave, B's statement must be false. B said "A and C are of the same type." For this to be false, A and C must be of DIFFERENT types. A is a Knight, so C must be a Knave. Now examine C: C is a Knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. But A IS a knight, so the statement is true — contradiction (a Knave cannot make a true statement). Therefore Case 1 is impossible. A cannot be a Knight.

Case 2: Suppose A is a Knave. Then A's statement "B is a knave" is false, so B is NOT a knave → B is a Knight. Since B is a Knight, B's statement "A and C are of the same type" is true. A is a Knave, so for A and C to be the same type, C must also be a Knave. Now examine C: C is a Knave, so C's statement "A is a knight" must be false. A is indeed a Knave, so "A is a knight" is false — consistent. All three statements are satisfied.

To exclude every alternative explicitly, enumerate all 8 assignments (K=Knight, N=Knave):
1. K,K,K: A says "B knave" → false, but A is K → impossible.
2. K,K,N: A says "B knave" → false, but A is K → impossible.
3. K,N,K: A true (B is N ✓). B says "A,C same": A=K,C=K same → true, but B is N must lie → impossible.
4. K,N,N: A true (B is N ✓). B says "A,C same": A=K,C=N different → false ✓ (B lies). C says "A knight": true, but C is N must lie → impossible.
5. N,K,K: A says "B knave": B=K so false ✓ (A lies). B says "A,C same": A=N,C=K different → false, but B is K must tell truth → impossible.
6. N,K,N: A says "B knave": B=K so false ✓. B says "A,C same": A=N,C=N same → true ✓. C says "A knight": A=N so false ✓. CONSISTENT.
7. N,N,K: A says "B knave": B=N true, but A is N must lie → impossible.
8. N,N,N: A says "B knave": B=N true, but A is N must lie → impossible.

Only assignment 6 works: A = Knave, B = Knight, C = Knave.

【答案】
A 是无赖（Knave），B 是骑士（Knight），C 是无赖（Knave）。

推理：假设 A 是骑士，则 A 说"B 是无赖"为真，故 B 是无赖；B 说谎，其" A 与 C 同类"为假，故 A、C 不同类，A 是骑士则 C 是无赖；但 C 是无赖，其" A 是骑士"必为假，而 A 实为骑士，矛盾。故 A 不能是骑士，只能是无赖。A 是无赖 → "B 是无赖"为假 → B 是骑士；B 说真话"A 与 C 同类"为真，A 是无赖故 C 也是无赖；C 是无赖，"A 是骑士"为假，自洽。枚举全部 8 种身份组合，仅有 (A=无赖, B=骑士, C=无赖) 一种不矛盾。

## H3

【思考】
Consider (A) "He didn't reply." and (B) "He didn't bother to reply."

(a) Literal propositional content: Both sentences have the SAME truth-conditional content — they both assert that he did not reply. There is no difference in what is literally claimed to be true or false. The difference lies in the speaker's stance/attitude: (A) is neutral and merely reports the absence of a reply; (B), by adding "bother to", conveys that the speaker regards the reply as a minimal, expected, easily-performed courtesy, and that the failure to reply reflects the subject's negligence, dismissiveness, or disrespect. (B) is evaluative; (A) is not.

(b) Presupposition carried by "bother to": The verb "bother (to)" presupposes that the action it modifies was a normal, minimal, and reasonably expected thing to do — a small effort that one could and (in the speaker's view) should have made. This presupposition survives negation (it is part of what "bother" contributes regardless of whether the action happened). In other words, "He didn't bother to reply" presupposes that replying was a low-effort, expected/owed behavior.

Implicature (conversational): By choosing "bother to", the speaker implicates a negative evaluation of the subject — that he was lazy, inconsiderate, or deliberately dismissive; that the silence was an easy-to-avoid choice rather than an inability; and that the speaker is criticizing him. This is a conversational implicature arising from the choice of wording, not a presupposition.

(c) Why it cannot describe something the speaker thinks "should not have been expected": Because "bother to" presupposes that the action was a minimal, expected/owed courtesy. If the speaker believes the reply was NOT to be expected (e.g., the person was in a coma, or was a complete stranger with no reason to answer, or the situation gave no ground for a reply), then asserting he "didn't bother to reply" is pragmatically infelicitous — it falsely presupposes an expectation or obligation that does not hold. Example: A man has been in a coma for a week. You would NOT say "He didn't bother to reply to my email" (that wrongly presupposes he should/could easily have replied); you would say "He didn't reply" (neutral) or "He was unable to reply." Thus "bother to" is incompatible with describing a non-expected event.

【答案】
(a) 字面命题内容无差别：两句都断言"他没有回复"，真值条件相同。差别在说话人立场：(A) 中性陈述"未回复"这一事实；(B) 加 "bother to" 后带有评价立场，暗示回复是举手之劳、理应做到，而对方未回复是怠慢、轻慢或不屑的表现。

(b) 预设（presupposition）："bother to" 预设该动作是日常、省力、理应/可期待做到的小事（即"回复"本是被期待的、轻松的礼貌行为），此预设不被否定取消。隐含（implicature）：说话人由此隐含对当事人的负面评价——他懒惰、不体贴或刻意冷落，沉默是轻易可避免的选择，且说话人在批评他。

(c) 不能用于"本就不该期待发生"的事，因为 "bother to" 预设该动作是省力且被期待/应尽的行为。若说话人认为回复根本不该被期待（如对方已昏迷一周，或对方是毫无理由回复的陌生人），说 "He didn't bother to reply" 就错误地预设了"本应/本可轻松回复"这一期待，语用上不成立。例如：对昏迷者不会说 "He didn't bother to reply to my email"，而会说 "He didn't reply" 或 "He was unable to reply"。

## H2

【思考】
We form 7-digit numbers using each of the digits 1,2,3,4,5,6,7 exactly once (all digits distinct, no zero). Count those divisible by 11.

Divisibility by 11 rule: A number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11, i.e., (sum of digits in odd positions) − (sum of digits in even positions) ≡ 0 (mod 11). For a 7-digit number with positions 1..7, the odd positions are {1,3,5,7} (four positions) and the even positions are {2,4,6} (three positions). Note that counting from the left or from the right gives the same parity assignment for a 7-digit number, so the rule is consistent.

Let S_odd = sum of the 4 digits in odd positions, S_even = sum of the 3 digits in even positions.
Total sum of digits = 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28.
So S_odd + S_even = 28.
Divisibility requires S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).

Now S_odd − S_even = (S_odd + S_even) − 2·S_even = 28 − 2·S_even, which is always even. Also S_odd − S_even = 2·S_odd − 28, also even. Multiples of 11 that are even are 0, ±22, ±44,... 
Bounds: S_odd is the sum of 4 distinct digits from 1..7. Minimum = 1+2+3+4 = 10; maximum = 4+5+6+7 = 22. Thus S_odd − S_even = 2·S_odd − 28 ranges from 2·10−28 = −8 to 2·22−28 = 16. In the interval [−8, 16], the only multiple of 11 achievable is 0 (since 11 would require 2·S_odd = 39 → S_odd = 19.5, not an integer; −11 is below −8). Therefore we must have S_odd − S_even = 0, i.e., S_odd = S_even = 14.

So we need to choose 4 digits (for the odd positions) whose sum is 14; the remaining 3 digits automatically sum to 28−14 = 14 for the even positions.

Enumerate all 4-element subsets of {1,2,3,4,5,6,7} summing to 14:
- {1,2,4,7}: 1+2+4+7 = 14
- {1,2,5,6}: 1+2+5+6 = 14
- {1,3,4,6}: 1+3+4+6 = 14
- {2,3,4,5}: 2+3+4+5 = 14
These are exactly 4 subsets. (Equivalently, the 3-element subsets summing to 14 are {1,6,7},{2,5,7},{3,4,7},{3,5,6}, whose complements are the four 4-subsets above — confirming the count of 4.)

For each such choice:
- The 4 selected digits can be arranged among the 4 odd positions in 4! = 24 ways.
- The remaining 3 digits can be arranged among the 3 even positions in 3! = 6 ways.

So each subset yields 24 × 6 = 144 valid numbers. With 4 subsets, total = 4 × 144 = 576.

Therefore, 576 seven-digit numbers formed from 1..7 (each used once) are divisible by 11.

【答案】
共有 **576** 个。

推导：被 11 整除的判别法为"奇数位数字之和 − 偶数位数字之和"是 11 的倍数。七位数位置 1–7 中，奇数位有 4 个（1,3,5,7），偶数位有 3 个（2,4,6）。设奇数位和为 S_odd、偶数位和为 S_even，则 S_odd + S_even = 1+2+…+7 = 28，且要求 S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11)。因 S_odd − S_even = 2·S_odd − 28 恒为偶数，而 S_odd 取值在 10 到 22 之间，故 S_odd − S_even 在 [−8,16] 内，其中 11 的倍数只有 0（11 需 S_odd=19.5 非整数，−11 超出范围）。所以必须 S_odd = S_even = 14。从 {1..7} 取 4 个数和为 14 的子集恰有 4 个：{1,2,4,7}、{1,2,5,6}、{1,3,4,6}、{2,3,4,5}。每个子集对应：4 个奇数位排列 4! = 24 种，剩余 3 个偶数位排列 3! = 6 种，共 24×6 = 144 个；4 个子集合计 4×144 = 576。

## H1

【思考】
The line "王顾左右而言他" comes from the Mencius (《孟子·梁惠王下》). I analyze the word "他".

(a) Meaning of "他" in this sentence: It means "别的（事情）" / "其他的事" — "other things / something else". The king looked to his left and right (at his attendants) and spoke of other matters, i.e., he changed the subject / evaded the topic.

(b) Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "he/him": In Pre-Qin Chinese, "他" did NOT function as a third-person pronoun. The third-person pronouns of classical Chinese were 之 (used as object), 其 (used as possessive / sometimes subject), and 彼. The word "他" originally meant "别的、其他的" (other, another). The use of "他" as a third-person pronoun developed much later (its pronominal use became common only in the medieval/vernacular period and especially modern Mandarin). In the Mencius, "言他" = "言他事" = "speak of other matters." The character was sometimes written as 佗 or 它, both meaning "other."

(c) Concrete deviation if read as modern "him/her": If "他" were the third-person pronoun, the sentence would mean "The king looked left and right and spoke TO / ABOUT him (a third party)" — i.e., the king turned to address or mention some other person. But that misrepresents the anecdote. The original point is that the king, unable to answer Mencius's moral challenge, deflected by glancing around and talking about something else — illustrating evasion / changing the subject. Reading "他" as a person wrongly introduces a referent (a third party) that does not exist in the context and loses the "evading the topic" nuance that is the whole point of the story.

【答案】
(a) "他" 的意思是"别的（事情）"、"其他的事"，即"言他"= 说别的事、岔开话题。整句是说：王看了看左右（侍从），说起别的事情来（避而不答）。

(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词"他"，因为在先秦汉语中"他"并不作第三人称代词。先秦的第三人称代词是"之"（宾语）、"其"（定语/主语）、"彼"等；"他"本义为"别的、其他的"。直到中古以后、特别是近代白话/现代汉语里，"他"才逐渐用作第三人称代词。在《孟子》语境中"言他"即"言他事"（说别的事）。

(c) 若按"第三人称代词"理解，整句会变成"王看看左右，对他（某个第三者）说话/谈起他"，即王转去对/谈某个旁人。这就歪曲了原意：原文要表达的是王答不上孟子接连的诘问，便左顾右盼、把话题岔到别的事上，体现的是"避而不答、转移话题"。把"他"当成"人"，会凭空引入一个上下文并不存在的第三者指称，并丢失"王在回避主题"这一全句核心意涵。
