## H6

【思考】
I examine each of the six learner sentences and determine whether it is natural/correct in standard English, and if not, identify the error type and provide the correction.

(1) "I'm looking forward to meet you." The phrase "look forward to" is a fixed expression in which "to" is a preposition, not an infinitive marker. Prepositions must be followed by a noun phrase or a gerund (the -ing form). Therefore the bare infinitive "meet" is ungrammatical. Correction: "I'm looking forward to meeting you."

(2) "She suggested me to take the train." The verb "suggest" does not take the pattern "suggest + indirect object + to-infinitive." That pattern belongs to verbs like "advise," "persuade," "tell." Instead "suggest" takes either a direct object (a noun/gerund), a that-clause, or "suggest something to someone." So "suggest me to do" is an error of verb complementation. Corrections: "She suggested (that) I take the train," or "She suggested taking the train," or "She suggested the train to me."

(3) "Despite of the rain, we went out." "Despite" is already a preposition meaning "without being affected by"; it does not take the particle "of." The "of" is required only with the synonymous phrase "in spite of." So "despite of" is a morphological/collocation error. Correction: "Despite the rain, we went out" (or "In spite of the rain, we went out").

(4) "He's capable to do it." The adjective "capable" is followed by the preposition "of" plus a gerund, not by a to-infinitive. So "capable to" is an error of adjective complementation. Correction: "He's capable of doing it."

(5) "I would rather stay than to go." After the correlative "would rather ... than ...," both verbs must be bare infinitives (no "to"). The "to" before "go" is therefore ungrammatical. Correction: "I would rather stay than go."

(6) "It's worth to try." The adjective "worth" is followed by a gerund, not a to-infinitive. Correction: "It's worth trying." (One can also say "It's worth a try" with a noun.)

【答案】
逐句判断如下：
(1) 错误。"look forward to" 中的 to 是介词，后接动名词，不能接不定式。正确表达：I'm looking forward to meeting you.
(2) 错误。动词 suggest 不能接"间接宾语 + to do"结构（那是 advise/tell 等动词的用法）。应改为：She suggested (that) I take the train. 或 She suggested taking the train.
(3) 错误。despite 本身是介词，后面不能加 of（of 只与 in spite of 搭配）。正确表达：Despite the rain, we went out.
(4) 错误。capable 后接 of + 动名词，不接不定式。正确表达：He's capable of doing it.
(5) 错误。would rather ... than ... 后面两个动词都用原形（不带 to）。正确表达：I would rather stay than go.
(6) 错误。worth 后接动名词，不接不定式。正确表达：It's worth trying.

## H5

【思考】
The sentence is "他谁都不认识." I analyze its possible readings, the syntactic source of ambiguity, and compare it with "他谁都认识."

(a) Possible readings. The string consists of 他 + 谁都 + 不 + 认识. Because Chinese lacks morphological case marking and allows object preposing, 他 can be parsed either as the subject or as a fronted object. The word 谁 in the "谁都" construction is a 任指 (free/categorical indefinite) pronoun meaning "anyone/anybody," not a question word and not a 虚指 (vague) use.
Reading A (他 as subject): 他 [谁都不认识] = "He does not know anyone" = "He knows nobody." Here 谁 is the object, 任指.
Reading B (他 as fronted object): [谁都不认识 他] = "Nobody knows him." Here 谁 is the subject, 任指, and 他 is the object that has been preposed to topic position.
In both readings 谁 is 任指.

(b) Root of the ambiguity. It is a scope/parsing ambiguity caused by (i) the absence of case morphology, so 他 can be subject or object; (ii) the possibility of preposing the object to a pre-verbal topic position; and (iii) the universal-negative "谁都…不…" construction, in which 谁 functions as a 任指 quantifier whose scope can attach to either the subject or the object slot. The surface word order does not uniquely determine grammatical relations.

(c) Comparison with "他谁都认识." That sentence is also ambiguous between "He knows everyone" (他 subject) and "Everyone knows him" (他 object). Structurally the ambiguity is of the same type. However, the two sentences differ in pragmatic salience. In the affirmative "他谁都认识," the two readings are about equally available. In the negative "他谁都不认识," the reading with 他 as subject ("He knows nobody") is strongly dominant, while the reading with 他 as object ("Nobody knows him") is much less natural, because a negative universal 谁…不… strongly prefers 谁 to be interpreted as the subject. Thus the negative preserves the same structural ambiguity but is more asymmetric in acceptability, whereas the affirmative is more balanced.

【答案】
(a) 这句话至少有两种理解：
理解一（他作主语）："他谁都不认识" = 他不认识任何人 / 他谁也不认识。句中"谁"是任指用法（"任何人"），作动词"认识"的宾语。
理解二（他作前置宾语）：相当于"谁都不认识他" = 没有人认识他。句中"谁"仍是任指用法，作主语，"他"是被前置的话题宾语。
两种理解里"谁"都是任指（不是疑问，也不是虚指）。
(b) 歧义的根源在于汉语缺乏形态格标记，主语/宾语无法靠词形区分；同时宾语可以话题化前置到动词之前；再加上"谁都……不……"这一任指全称否定构式，"谁"的辖域既可以落在主语位也可以落在宾语位，表层语序不能唯一确定语法关系，从而产生结构歧义。
(c) "他谁都认识"同样有歧义（"他认识所有人" / "所有人都认识他"），歧义类型相同。但两者歧义程度不完全相同：肯定句"他谁都认识"两种理解都比较自然、对称；而否定句"他谁都不认识"中"他作主语（他不认识任何人）"这一理解占绝对优势，"他作宾语（没人认识他）"那一种明显不自然，因为"谁……都不……"这种否定全称构式强烈倾向于把"谁"理解为施事主语。所以否定句保留了同样的结构歧义，但两种理解的可接受度更不对称。

## H4

【思考】
Three people A, B, C; each is a knight (always tells truth) or a knave (always lies).
Statements:
A: "B is a knave."
B: "A and C are of the same type."
C: "A is a knight."

I consider the two possible types of A and eliminate the impossible one.

Case 1: Suppose A is a knight. Then A's statement is true, so B is a knave. Since B is a knave, B's statement is false. B said "A and C are of the same type"; this being false means A and C are NOT of the same type. A is a knight, so C must be a knave. Now check C: C is a knave, so C's statement must be false. C said "A is a knight." But A is indeed a knight, so C's statement is true — contradiction, because a knave cannot utter a true statement. Therefore Case 1 is impossible; A cannot be a knight.

Case 2: Suppose A is a knave. Then A's statement is false. A said "B is a knave," so the falsity means B is NOT a knave, i.e., B is a knight. Since B is a knight, B's statement is true. B said "A and C are of the same type"; A is a knave, so C must also be a knave (same type). Now check C: C is a knave, so C's statement must be false. C said "A is a knight." A is a knave, so "A is a knight" is indeed false — consistent. No contradiction.

Thus the only consistent assignment is A = knave, B = knight, C = knave. This is unique because A has only two possibilities, and the knight case was ruled out, forcing the knave case and consequently the unique identities of B and C.

【答案】
唯一自洽的分配是：A 是无赖，B 是骑士，C 是无赖。

推理过程：
先假设 A 是骑士。则 A 说真话，"B 是无赖"为真，故 B 是无赖。B 说谎，其"我和 C 同类"为假，即 A、C 不同类；A 是骑士，所以 C 是无赖。但 C 若为无赖，其" A 是骑士"应为假，可 A 确是骑士，这句话为真，矛盾。故 A 不可能是骑士。
因此 A 必为无赖。A 说谎，"B 是无赖"为假，故 B 是骑士。B 说真话，"A 和 C 同类"为真；A 是无赖，故 C 也是无赖。检验 C：C 是无赖，其" A 是骑士"应为假，而 A 确为无赖，这句话为假，符合。
所以 A=无赖、B=骑士、C=无赖，且唯一。

## H3

【思考】
Compare (A) "He didn't reply." and (B) "He didn't bother to reply."

(a) Literal propositional content: both sentences assert the same truth-conditional proposition — that he did not produce a reply. There is no difference in what is literally claimed to be true. The difference lies in the speaker's stance/attitude. (A) is neutral and merely reports the absence of a reply. (B) conveys that the speaker views the non-reply as a failure to expend even a small amount of effort, implying disregard, laziness, or rudeness on the part of "him."

(b) Presupposition and implicature of adding "bother to." The phrase "bother to V" presupposes that V was a low-cost, trivially easy action available to the agent, and that there was at least a weak possibility or expectation that the agent might perform it. It implicates (via conversational implicature) that the agent was inconsiderate, dismissive, or lazy — that he chose not to make even a minimal effort that a reasonable person would have made. So "He didn't bother to reply" presupposes that replying was easy for him and (at least possibly) expected, and implicates that his silence reflects a lack of care.

(c) Why it cannot describe something the speaker thinks "should not have been expected anyway." Because "bother to V" presupposes the action was a minimal, available, and at least weakly expected effort. If the speaker believes the action was not to be expected at all, using "didn't bother to" faults the agent for omitting something the speaker themselves thinks wasn't owed or anticipated, creating a pragmatic clash. Example: "The baby didn't bother to reply to my question." A baby is not expected to reply, so it is infelicitous to say it "didn't bother to reply" — that phrasing implies the baby should have made a trivial effort. Similarly, "The comatose patient didn't bother to attend the meeting" is odd if one believes a comatose person could not be expected to attend. The construction is only appropriate when the speaker regards the action as a small, expected effort that the agent skipped.

【答案】
(a) 两句话的字面命题内容没有差别——都断言"他没有回复"这一事实。差别在说话人立场：(A) 中性报道"他没回复"；(B) 带有说话人态度，暗示"他连这点小事都不肯费心"，含有怠慢、懒散或无礼的意味。
(b) 加上 "bother to" 后，这句话预设（presuppose）：回复对"他"而言是轻而易举、代价极小且（至少有可能）被期待做的动作；它隐含（implicate）："他"漠不关心、怠慢或偷懒，连本可顺手做的小事都不肯做。
(c) 因为 "bother to V" 预设该动作是微小、可行且（至少弱）被期待的努力。若说话人认为某事本就不该被期待发生，再用 "didn't bother to" 就等于指责当事人没做一件说话人自己都认为不该期待的事，产生语用冲突。例如：说 "The baby didn't bother to reply"（婴儿根本不会被期待会回答），就很不自然；又如 "The comatose patient didn't bother to attend the meeting"（昏迷病人本就不可能被期待出席），同样别扭。该结构只适用于说话人认为那是件微小、本应/可被期待却被略过的动作。

## H2

【思考】
We form 7-digit numbers using digits 1 through 7, each exactly once. We count those divisible by 11.

Divisibility by 11 rule: a number is divisible by 11 iff the alternating sum of its digits is a multiple of 11. For a 7-digit number with digits d1 d2 d3 d4 d5 d6 d7 (d1 most significant), the test is (d1 + d3 + d5 + d7) − (d2 + d4 + d6) ≡ 0 (mod 11).

Let S_odd = d1 + d3 + d5 + d7 (the four odd-position digits) and S_even = d2 + d4 + d6 (the three even-position digits). The total sum of all digits is 1+2+3+4+5+6+7 = 28, so S_odd + S_even = 28. We need S_odd − S_even ≡ 0 (mod 11).

Express D = S_odd − S_even = S_odd − (28 − S_odd) = 2·S_odd − 28.
S_odd is the sum of 4 distinct digits from {1,…,7}. Minimum S_odd = 1+2+3+4 = 10; maximum S_odd = 4+5+6+7 = 22. Hence D ranges from 2·10−28 = −8 to 2·22−28 = 16. The multiples of 11 within [−8, 16] are 0 and 11 (note −11 is below −8).
- D = 11 gives 2·S_odd − 28 = 11 → S_odd = 39/2 = 19.5, not an integer, impossible.
- D = 0 gives 2·S_odd − 28 = 0 → S_odd = 14, and then S_even = 14.

So the only viable condition is S_odd = 14 (the four odd positions sum to 14; the three even positions then also sum to 14).

Now enumerate the 4-element subsets of {1,…,7} whose sum is 14:
{1,2,4,7} = 14
{1,2,5,6} = 14
{1,3,4,6} = 14
{2,3,4,5} = 14
These are the only four (verified by exhaustive checking: any other 4-subset either repeats or fails to sum to 14).

For each such subset:
- The 4 chosen digits occupy the 4 odd positions (1,3,5,7) in any order: 4! = 24 ways.
- The remaining 3 digits occupy the 3 even positions (2,4,6) in any order: 3! = 6 ways.
So each subset yields 24 × 6 = 144 numbers.

Total = 4 × 144 = 576.

(No leading-zero concern since all digits are 1–7, so every arrangement is a valid 7-digit number.)

【答案】
能被 11 整除的七位数共有 576 个。

推导：被 11 整除的判别法是奇数位数字和与偶数位数字和之差是 11 的倍数。设奇数位（第1、3、5、7位）数字和为 S_奇，偶数位（第2、4、6位）为 S_偶。全部数字和 1+2+3+4+5+6+7=28，故 S_奇+S_偶=28，且要求 S_奇−S_偶 ≡0 (mod 11)。
令 D = S_奇−S_偶 = 2·S_奇−28。S_奇是 4 个不同数字之和，范围 10~22，故 D 范围 [−8,16]，其中 11 的倍数只有 0 和 11。D=11 得 S_奇=19.5（非整数，舍去）；D=0 得 S_奇=14，相应 S_偶=14。
从 {1..7} 中取 4 个数和为 14 的子集只有 4 个：{1,2,4,7}、{1,2,5,6}、{1,3,4,6}、{2,3,4,5}。
每个子集：4 个奇数位可任意排列（4!=24 种），剩余 3 个偶数位任意排列（3!=6 种），共 24×6=144 个。总数 = 4×144 = 576。

## H1

【思考】
The sentence from Mencius: "王顾左右而言他。"

(a) Meaning of "他" in this context. In pre-Qin Chinese, "他" (often written 他/佗/它) meant "别的、其他的" (other, different), functioning as a determiner/adjective meaning "other." So "言他" means "speak of other things / change the subject." The king looked left and right and spoke of other matters, deflecting Mencius's pointed question.

(b) Why it cannot be the modern third-person pronoun "he/him." The third-person pronoun usage of "他" developed only later in Chinese (roughly from the 魏晋南北朝 / Tang period onward; in pre-Qin texts "他" had not yet grammaticalized as a personal pronoun). In the classical period "他" uniformly meant "other/different." Reading it as the modern pronoun "tā" (= he/him) imposes a later grammaticalization onto an earlier text, which is anachronistic.

(c) Concrete error if read as third-person pronoun. If "他" were the third-person pronoun, "言他" would mean "spoke of him" (some specific male third party), giving "The king looked left and right and spoke of him." This is wrong in two ways: (i) it invents a spurious referent ("him") that does not exist in the passage — the king is evading by switching to entirely different topics, not talking about a particular person; (ii) it destroys the rhetorical point of the anecdote, where the king, unable to answer Mencius's questions about official responsibility, deliberately changes the subject. The intended meaning "speaking of other things / changing the subject" is lost. Evidence from pre-Qin usage: in the Shijing, "它山之石，可以攻玉" uses 它 (=他) meaning "other mountain's," confirming the old sense "other." Thus in 先秦, 他 = 别的/其他的, not a personal pronoun.

【答案】
(a) "他"在这里的意思是"别的、其他的（事情）"，"言他"即"说别的事 / 转移话题"。整句意为：大王环顾左右，说起别的事情（岔开话题）。
(b) 不能理解为现代汉语第三人称代词"他"，是因为在先秦汉语中"他"尚未语法化为第三人称代词（"他"作人称代词是后世，约魏晋南北朝至唐代才逐渐发展起来的用法）。先秦的"他"（也写作佗、它）意为"别的、其他的"，是形容词/限定词用法。
(c) 若按第三人称代词理解，"言他"会变成"说他（某个男性第三人）"，整句被误读为"大王环顾左右，说起了他"。这产生两处具体偏差：一是凭空制造了一个原文并不存在的指称对象（"他"这个人），而原文是大王用别的话题来回避，并非在谈某个特定的人；二是破坏了文意的修辞重点——孟子层层追问责任，大王答不上来便故意岔开话题，若解为"说他"就丢失了"转移话题"这一核心意思。先秦用例可佐证：如《诗经》"它山之石，可以攻玉"中"它"（=他）即"别的（山）"，可见先秦"他"义为"别的/其他的"，并非人称代词。
